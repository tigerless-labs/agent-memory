The eight Jhanas are progressive states of meditation in Buddhist practice, organized in two groups:

**First Four Jhanas (Fine-Material Realm):**
1. First Jhana: Strong sustained focus on single meditation object (breath, mantra); suppression of distractions
2. Second Jhana: Deep joy and contentment; focus maintained with reduced effort
3. Third Jhana: Equanimity and balance; deeper inner peace and freedom from distractions
4. Fourth Jhana: Pure awareness and detachment; blissful serenity; complete freedom from distraction

**Fifth through Eighth Jhanas (Immaterial Realm):**
5. Fifth Jhana: Sense of infinite space; experience of consciousness expanding beyond body boundaries
6. Sixth Jhana: Sense of infinite consciousness; dissolution of separateness between observer and observed; unity experience
7. Seventh Jhana: Sense of infinite bliss; pure, unbounded joy; freedom from negative thought/emotion
8. Eighth Jhana: Sense of infinite peace; complete stillness of mind; profound inner peace and serenity

Each level builds on the previous, deepening concentration (samadhi) and leading to transformation of mind and spiritual insight.
