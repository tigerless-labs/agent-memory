**Wagashi (和菓子)** - Traditional Japanese sweets/confections

History: Dates back to Heian period (794-1185 CE), originally introduced from China, developed into unique Japanese art form reflecting aesthetic values of simplicity, elegance, and harmony with nature. Often served at Japanese tea ceremony or during special occasions.

**Main Types of Wagashi:**
1. **Namagashi** - Fresh sweets made from seasonal ingredients, often served at tea ceremony
2. **Higashi** - Dry sweets made from sugar, rice flour, or other ingredients, dried to preserve
3. **Mochi** - Rice flour-based sweets, often filled with sweet fillings

**Wagashi Recipes to Try:**
1. **Mochi Wagashi** - Traditional mochi using glutinous rice flour and sweet fillings (red bean paste or sweetened chestnut)
2. **Sakura Mochi** - Cherry blossom-shaped mochi with sweetened red bean paste, typically served during Hanami (cherry blossom viewing)
3. **Yokan** - Jellied dessert made from red bean paste, agar, and sugar

**Tips for Making Wagashi:**
- Use seasonal ingredients to reflect changing seasons
- Pay attention to presentation, shape, and color (aesthetic is as important as taste)
- Balance flavors - keep sweetness subtle and delicate, balance with salt, umami, or acidity

**Related Recipes:**
- **Mochi** - Made from 2 cups glutinous rice flour, 1 cup water, 1/2 cup granulated sugar, with filling of choice
- **Manju** - Steamed buns with sweet bean paste (azuki beans), made from all-purpose flour, sugar, water, salt
