**Authors:** Diego Blas and Alexander C. Jenkins  
**Institutions:** Universitat Autònoma de Barcelona, Institut de Física d'Altes Energies (IFAE), King's College London  
**Published:** Physical Review D (Feb 8, 2023)

**Key insight:** Proposes using existing astronomical binary systems (specifically Earth-Moon) to detect stochastic gravitational waves in the microhertz frequency gap, which currently lacks detector coverage (between pulsar timing arrays at nanohertz and LISA at millihertz).

**Method:** Exploits resonant response when gravitational wave frequency matches binary orbital frequency. Effects accumulate over time for persistent stochastic backgrounds.

**Earth-Moon application:** 
- Orbital period ~28 days matches microhertz frequency requirement
- Moon hosts five Apollo retroreflectors (50+ years of laser ranging data)
- Required measurement precision: ~0.1 mm for Earth-Moon distance
- Potentially achievable with current technology

**Significance:** Could bridge major detector gap using existing infrastructure rather than building new observatories on astronomical scales.
