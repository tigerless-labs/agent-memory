Section 127(4) of the Corporations Act 2001 (Australia) covers flexibility in how companies may execute documents, including deeds.

INTERPRETATION: Three alternative valid execution methods per case law:
1) Common law method of executing a deed
2) Constitutional method prescribed by company constitution
3) Estoppel - where deed is binding on company via estoppel

Key principle: No particular formality required for corporate signature (Ann Lyons J, Sunbay Projects).

PRIMARY CASES:
- Prime Constructions Pty Ltd v Westbridge Investments Pty Ltd: Young CJ in Eq outlined s 127(4); deed enforced despite technical defects via estoppel
- Boz One Pty Ltd v McLellan [2015] VSCA 68; 105 ACSR 325 (para 206): synthesizes multiple interpretations
- Guang Dong Zhi Gao Australia Pty Ltd v Fortuna Network Pty Ltd: Einstein J approved Young CJ's interpretation
- Sunbay Projects Pty Ltd v PR Wieland Holdings Pty Ltd: Ann Lyons J on no formality requirement

A technically defective deed can still bind a company if it satisfies one of the s 127(4) alternative methods.
