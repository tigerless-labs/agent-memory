Judy Chicago (born Judith Sylvia Cohen, 1939) is a pioneering feminist artist.

Education: University of Illinois, Art Institute of Chicago, UCLA (MFA 1964)

Major Works:
- The Dinner Party (1974-1979): Monumental installation celebrating 39 women from history and mythology. Features triangular table with 39 place settings, ceramic plates with vulva-like forms symbolizing female sexuality and creativity. Now in permanent collection at Brooklyn Museum.
- Atmospheric Sculptures (1960s-1970s): Environmental installations using colored smoke and lights
- Birth Project (1980-1985): Paintings, weavings, sculptures exploring birth and motherhood themes
- PowerPlay (1982-1987): Paintings and drawings examining power dynamics and relationships

Teaching & Activism: Taught at CalArts, UCLA, Duke University. Advocate for women's rights, LGBTQ+ rights, environmental issues.

Significance: Key figure in 1970s feminist art movement challenging patriarchal art world structures. Addressed female sexuality, reproductive rights, erasure of women's contributions to history.

Legacy: Inspired countless feminist artists. Awarded National Medal of Arts (2018), highest US honor for artists. Her work continues to inspire globally.
