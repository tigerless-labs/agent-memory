Framework to help teams determine whether a property appraisal order meets complexity criteria and justifies higher fees.

## Five assessment categories:

1. **Location** — Remote areas, unique environmental/geographical features (hillside, water, wooded), high crime, environmental hazards, challenging street layout

2. **Property type and design** — Unusual/complex designs (multi-unit, multiple levels), special features (pools, hot tubs, decks), complex mechanical systems (geothermal, radiant heat), outbuildings

3. **Age and condition** — Significantly older/newer than area typical, extensive repairs needed, significant wear/tear (water damage, pest infestation), major alterations/conversions

4. **Zoning and land use** — Unique zoning restrictions, floodplain/conservation area/historic district locations, complex regulations, HOA restrictions, wetlands designation, zoning variances or conditional use permits

5. **Market conditions** — Rapidly appreciating or declining markets, unique economic demographics (vacation homes, high-end properties), unemployment/foreclosure trends, unusual supply/demand factors

## Purpose:
Enable consistent, objective assessment of properties to justify fees, improve appraisal accuracy, reduce errors, and maintain client confidence.
