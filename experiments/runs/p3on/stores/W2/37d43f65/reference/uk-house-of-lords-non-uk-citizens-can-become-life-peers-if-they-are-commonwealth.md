Non-UK citizens can be appointed as life peers in the UK House of Lords if they meet these criteria:

- British citizens
- Commonwealth citizens  
- Republic of Ireland citizens
- Must have permanent residence in the UK

It is rare for non-UK citizens to be nominated or appointed.

Examples mentioned: Baroness Boothroyd, Baroness Hayman, Lord Patten of Barnes, Baroness Manningham-Buller.

The House of Lords has become increasingly diverse in recent years with more appointments of women, people from ethnic minorities, and individuals from non-traditional backgrounds, which strengthens its scrutiny of legislation.
