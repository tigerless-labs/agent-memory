## Protagonist
**Lyra** — A skilled memory thief with fragmented or missing memories. Their identity is shrouded in mystery, and they lack clear recollection of their past.

## Setting & World
- Dystopian future where memories can be extracted, stored, and sold
- Advanced neurotechnology enables memory manipulation and transfer
- Memory Authority regulates the trade, but black markets and corruption thrive
- World emerged from a devastating war that destroyed most of humanity's collective memories

## Core Premise
Lyra is hired to steal memories from a powerful CEO. As they delve into the CEO's memories, they begin discovering clues about their own past and true identity, forcing them to confront questions about their authentic self.

## Central Themes
- **Identity**: What defines us when memories can be fabricated or altered?
- **Self-discovery**: Lyra's journey to uncover their true self and past
- **Authenticity**: Reality and authenticity of experiences and relationships when memories are unreliable
- **Trust and connection**: Learning to form genuine relationships despite uncertainty

## Key Plot Twists
1. Lyra discovers their true identity and the reason behind their memory loss, forcing re-evaluation of self and purpose
2. CEO's true intentions revealed, with consequences of memory theft challenging Lyra's understanding of their role and relationships

## Character Arc
Lyra evolves from a solitary, skilled thief to someone capable of vulnerability and connection. They learn to trust and form relationships. Final decision: continue as a memory thief or forge a new path after discovering their true identity.

## Romance Elements
- Slow-burn emotional connection with a mark (theft target)
- Relationship built on mutual respect, trust, and communication amid tension and conflict
- Explores authenticity of love when memories might be unreliable
- Avoids insta-love; focuses on emotional intimacy and partnership
- Both characters must have agency and drive the relationship forward

## Research Interests
- Character-driven narratives with contradictions and complexity
- World-building that enhances character development
- Intersectionality of identity across culture, species, background
- Using sci-fi elements to explore philosophical questions about self and identity
