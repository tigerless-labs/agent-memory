# Cross Symbol Across Ancient Cultures

## Egyptian (Ankh)
- Represented the four elements of nature and their corresponding deities
- Symbol of life and immortality
- Embodied balance and harmony
- Horizontal arm: earthly realm; vertical arm: spiritual realm
- Intersection: joining of two realms and balance between them
- Associated with regeneration and eternal life
- Believed to bestow life and immortality on those who possessed it

## Phoenician
- Associated with goddess Astarte (fertility, sexuality, love)
- Represented balance between earthly and spiritual realms
- Representation of the divine in the natural world
- Symbolized the four directions of the world
- Used in temples, altars, jewelry, pottery, textiles
- Astarte believed to control the four elements (earth, air, fire, water) and ensure their balance

## Greek
- Known as the Tau symbol
- Associated with god Tammuz (also worshipped in Babylonian culture)
- Represented resurrection and regeneration
- Believed to be the shape of the phallus of god Dionysus
- Used in architecture and art; believed to have sacred and protective power

## Roman
- Initially used as a symbol of punishment and crucifixion of criminals
- Later adopted by Christianity as central religious icon

## Christian
- Symbolizes sacrifice of Jesus Christ and his death and resurrection
- Horizontal bar: earthly realm; vertical bar: divine realm
- Represents unity of God and humanity through Jesus Christ
- Symbol of hope, forgiveness, and eternal life

## Other Traditions
- Judaism: represents the Tree of Life in Kabbalah
- Hinduism: represents intersection of physical and spiritual realms; associated with god Vishnu
- Buddhism: crossed arms represent four immeasurables (love, compassion, joy, equanimity)
