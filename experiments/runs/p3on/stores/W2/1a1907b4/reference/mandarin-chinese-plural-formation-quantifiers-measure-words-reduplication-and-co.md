Mandarin Chinese does not change noun forms for plurals like English does. Instead:\n\n1. **Context**: Numbers with nouns imply plurality (e.g., 'liǎng ge rén' = two people)\n2. **Quantifiers**: Measure words + quantity (e.g., 'yī běn shū' = one book, 'liǎng běn shū' = two books)\n3. **Measure words**: Specific classifiers for different noun types (e.g., 'zhī' for animals: 'liǎng zhī yá' = two ducks)\n4. **Reduplication**: Some nouns reduplicate for plurality (e.g., 'māma' = mom, 'māmama' = moms)\n\nThis reflects Mandarin's emphasis on context over explicit grammatical marking, unlike English.
