**The Alhambra** is an ancient fortress and palace complex in Granada, Spain, constructed during the Moorish reign in the 13th and 14th centuries.

**Name**: Derived from Arabic "Al-Hamra" ("the red one"), referring to the reddish hue of its walls and towers.

**Original purpose**: Built as a fortress to defend against Christian attacks; also served as a royal palace by the Nasrid dynasty, functioning as the residence of the sultan and his court and center of political/cultural activity during the Nasrid period.

**After Christian conquest (1492)**: Used as a royal palace by the Catholic monarchs with several additions made to the complex.

**Later history**: Fell into disrepair over centuries but was restored in the 19th and 20th centuries.

**Today**: One of Spain's most visited landmarks and recognized as a **UNESCO World Heritage Site**, standing as testament to the art, architecture, and culture of the Moorish period in Spain.
