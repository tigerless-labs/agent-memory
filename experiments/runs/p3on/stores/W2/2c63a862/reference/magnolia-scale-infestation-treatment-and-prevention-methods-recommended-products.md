## Prevention
- Regularly inspect magnolia trees for scale insects or sticky sap on leaves/branches
- Prune and dispose of infested branches
- Encourage natural predators (ladybugs, lacewings)
- Provide proper, balanced nutrition
- Avoid over-fertilizing
- Water trees deeply and infrequently to prevent stress

## Recommended Horticultural Products
1. **Neem oil** — Natural oil from neem tree; mix with water and spray per label
2. **Insecticidal soap** — Potassium salts of fatty acids; spray directly but rinse tree with water after to avoid leaf damage
3. **Dormant oil** — Heavy horticultural oil used in winter/early spring to smother eggs before hatch
4. **All Seasons Spray Oil** — Can be used year-round; mix with water and spray per label

## Spread & Control
- Scale insects excrete honeydew that attracts ants
- Ants **protect** scales from predators and transport them to other trees
- Crawlers can be carried by wind, bees, or butterflies
- Control ant populations with sticky barriers or baits to prevent spread

## Consequences if Untreated
- Infestation can last indefinitely
- Scales feed on tree sap, weakening and stunting growth
- Leaves turn yellow and drop prematurely; branches die back
- Can ultimately kill the tree, especially if already stressed
- Easier to control if caught early

## General Notes
- Always read and follow label instructions
- Rotate or combine different control methods to prevent pesticide resistance
- Insecticides should be last resort
