Straight photography emerged in the 1920s-1930s as a movement characterized by straightforward, objective approaches to photography without artistic manipulation or effects. 

**Key figures:**
- Alfred Stieglitz — coined the term, pioneered the style
- Edward Weston
- Paul Strand
- Ansel Adams

**Principles:** Rejected artificial effects and manipulation; sought to capture the world truthfully and honestly. Influenced by Pictorialism and documentary traditions, but distinguished by its commitment to honesty and clarity.

**Legacy:** Influences modern street photography, landscape photography, and portrait photography. Valued for commitment to objectivity and the power of photographic images to convey essence of the world.
