Assessment criteria for determining whether appraisal orders are complex and may justify higher fees:

**Location**
- Remote or limited-access areas
- Unique environmental/geographical features (hillside, water, wooded, etc.)
- High crime rate or environmental hazards
- Unusual street layout (cul-de-sac, steep driveway)

**Property type & design**
- Unusual/complex design (multi-unit, multiple levels, attached garage)
- Special features (swimming pool, hot tub, large deck)
- Unusual heating/cooling systems (geothermal, radiant heat)
- Outbuildings (barn, shed, guesthouse)

**Age & condition**
- Significantly older/newer than typical area properties
- Extensive repairs/renovations needed
- Significant wear (water damage, pest infestation)
- Major alterations (converted to multi-unit, additions/modifications)

**Zoning & land use**
- Unique zoning/restrictions (floodplain, conservation, historic district)
- Complex land use regulations (HOA rules, wetlands)
- Zoning variances or conditional use permits

**Market conditions**
- Rapidly appreciating or declining markets
- Unique characteristics (vacation homes, high-end properties)
- Unusual trends (high unemployment, foreclosures)
- Supply/demand factors (limited availability, specific feature demand)
