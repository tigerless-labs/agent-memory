**Clay Body Types & Properties**\n\n**Earthenware Clay**\n\nStrengths:\n- Easy to work with; soft and pliable\n- Low firing temperature (~1800°F/980°C)\n- Wide range of colorful glaze options\n\nWeaknesses:\n- Brittle and prone to cracking/breaking\n- Low strength and durability\n- Not suitable for functional ware; not food-safe\n\n**Air-Dry Clay**\n\nStrengths:\n- No kiln required; air dries naturally\n- Fast results; no lengthy firing process\n- Easy to repair; can be re-moistened and reshaped\n\nWeaknesses:\n- Limited durability; prone to cracking\n- Not suitable for outdoor use; susceptible to weathering\n- Best for small, simple projects only\n- Lacks strength of fired clay\n\n**Stoneware Clay**\n\nStrengths:\n- Strong and durable\n- High-temperature firing (~2400°F/1300°C) makes it resistant to thermal shock\n- Non-porous, food-safe, and watertight\n- Ideal for functional ware and outdoor use\n\nWeaknesses:\n- More challenging to work with; dense and less pliable\n- Requires high-temperature kiln access\n- Limited glaze options; requires high-temperature glazes\n- Steeper learning curve than earthenware
