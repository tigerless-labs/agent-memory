**Smoky Baked Beans Recipe**

** Servings: 8-10**

**Ingredients:**

* 1 pound dried navy beans, soaked overnight and drained
* 1 pound smoked bacon, cut into 1-inch pieces
* 1 medium onion, chopped
* 2 cloves of garlic, minced
* 1 cup ketchup
* 1/4 cup brown sugar
* 2 tablespoons apple cider vinegar
* 2 tablespoons Worcestershire sauce
* 1 teaspoon ground mustard
* 1 teaspoon smoked paprika (optional, but highly recommended for that smoky flavor)
* 1/4 teaspoon cayenne pepper (optional, for some heat)
* Salt and pepper, to taste
* 1/4 cup bourbon (optional, but trust me, it adds depth)

**Instructions:**

1. **Preheat your oven to 250°F (120°C).**
2. **Cook the bacon:** In a large skillet, cook the bacon over medium heat until crispy. Remove the bacon from the skillet with a slotted spoon and set aside on a paper towel-lined plate. Leave the drippings in the skillet.
3. **Soften the onion:** Add the chopped onion to the same skillet and cook until softened, about 5 minutes. Add the minced garlic and cook for an additional minute.
4. **Add the beans and spices:** Add the soaked and drained navy beans, ketchup, brown sugar, apple cider vinegar, Worcestershire sauce, ground mustard, smoked paprika, cayenne pepper (if using), salt, and pepper to the skillet. Stir to combine.
5. **Add the bacon and bourbon (if using):** Add the cooked bacon and bourbon (if using) to the skillet. Stir to combine.
6. **Transfer to a baking dish:** Transfer the bean mixture to a 9x13-inch baking dish.
7. **Bake low and slow:** Cover the dish with aluminum foil and bake for 6 hours. Remove the foil and continue baking for an additional 30 minutes to 1 hour, or until the beans are tender and the liquid has thickened.
8. **Let it rest:** Remove the dish from the oven and let it rest for at least 30 minutes before serving.

**Tips and Variations:**

* To add more smokiness, you can add 1-2 chipotle peppers in adobo sauce to the bean mixture.
* If you prefer a sweeter bean, reduce the amount of brown sugar or omit it altogether.
* To make this recipe in a slow cooker, cook on low for 8-10 hours.
* You can also add other ingredients like diced bell peppers, chopped jalapeños, or sliced hot dogs to the bean mixture for added flavor and texture.

These Smoky Baked Beans are perfect for your summer cookout, and they're sure to be a hit with your guests!

* Look for a whole packer brisket (10-12 pounds) or a flat cut brisket (5-6 pounds). The flat cut is leaner and more forgiving, but the packer brisket has more marbling, which means more flavor.
* Opt for a brisket with a thick fat cap (at least 1/4 inch) to keep the meat moist during the long cooking process.

**Preparation is key:**

* Trim excess fat from the brisket, if necessary, to ensure even cooking.
* Season the brisket generously with a dry rub, making sure to coat all surfaces evenly. Let it sit at room temperature for 1-2 hours to allow the seasonings to penetrate the meat.
* If you want to get fancy, inject the brisket with a marinade or mop sauce before smoking to add extra flavor.

**Low and slow is the way to go:**

* Set up your smoker to run at 225-250°F (110-120°C). You can use a charcoal, gas, or electric smoker, but wood-fired is ideal for that authentic flavor.
* Place the brisket in the smoker, fat side up, and close the lid. Smoke for 4-5 hours, or until the internal temperature reaches 160°F (71°C).
* After 4 hours, wrap the brisket in foil (the "Texas Crutch") to prevent drying out and promote tenderization. Continue smoking for another 2-3 hours, or until the internal temperature reaches 180-190°F (82-88°C).

**Resting is crucial:**

* Once the brisket reaches the desired temperature, remove it from the smoker and let it rest for 1-2 hours. This allows the juices to redistribute, making the meat even more tender.

**Slicing and serving:**

* Slice the brisket against the grain, using a sharp knife, to ensure tender and juicy slices.

**The Holy Trinity of BBQ Sauce:**

1. **Sweetness:** Provides depth and richness. Common sweeteners include brown sugar, honey, molasses, and maple syrup.
2. **Tanginess:** Adds brightness and acidity. Typical tangy ingredients are vinegar (apple cider, white wine, or balsamic), citrus juice (lemon or orange), and yogurt or sour cream.
3. **Heat:** Brings the spice and kick. Popular heat sources include hot sauce (Tabasco, Frank's RedHot, or Cholula), diced jalapeños or serrano peppers, and ground cayenne pepper or smoked paprika.

**Balancing the Flavors:**

1. **Start with a ratio:** Begin with a basic ratio of 2:1:1 (sweet:tangy:heat). Adjust to taste, as you prefer your sauce more sweet, tangy, or spicy.
2. **Layer the flavors:** Add ingredients in layers, allowing each to meld with the others. Start with the sweet components, followed by the tangy and heat elements.
3. **Acidity adjustment:** If your sauce is too sweet, add a splash of vinegar or citrus juice. If it's too acidic, add a bit more sweetener.
4. **Heat control:** If your sauce is too spicy, add a dairy product like sour cream or yogurt to temper the heat. If it's not spicy enough, add more hot sauce or peppers.
5. **Umami flavor:** Add a savory element, like soy sauce, Worcestershire sauce, or mushroom-based ingredients, to enhance the overall flavor profile.
6. **Thickeners:** Use tomato paste, ketchup, or cornstarch to achieve your desired consistency.
7. **Resting and aging:** Let your sauce sit for a few hours or overnight to allow the flavors to meld together. This will help you identify any imbalances and make adjustments.

**Tips and Variations:**

* **Regional twists:** Incorporate ingredients inspired by different BBQ styles, like Kansas City (tomato-based, sweet, and thick), Texas (beef-based, thin, and spicy), or Carolina (vinegar-based, tangy, and peppery).
* **Fruit additions:** Try adding fruit preserves, like peach or apricot, for a sweet and fruity twist.
* **Spice it up:** Experiment with unique spices, such as smoked paprika, ground coriander, or ground cinnamon, to create a signature flavor.
* **Bourbon or beer:** Add a splash of bourbon or beer to give your sauce a rich, depthful flavor.

Remember, the key to creating a great BBQ sauce is experimentation and patience. Don't be afraid to try new ingredients and adjust the flavors until you find the perfect balance for your taste buds. Happy sauce-making!
