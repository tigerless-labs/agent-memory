## Lighting

- Use natural light when possible; soft and flattering
- Position items near windows with diffused light or shoot during golden hour
- Use softbox or DIY diffuser (translucent umbrella or white fabric) to soften harsh shadows
- Avoid direct flash; use diffuser or bounce light instead
- Consider layered lighting: softbox for illumination + sidelight for texture/detail

## Composition

- Use plain, neutral backgrounds (white, gray, black) to focus attention on item
- Apply rule of thirds: divide image into thirds horizontally and vertically
- Experiment with unique angles: low shots, overhead, Dutch angles for visual interest
- Take close-ups of specific features to show condition and detail
- Show item in use (watch on wrist, guitar being played) to help buyers imagine ownership

## Editing

- Color correction: adjust temperature and saturation for accurate representation
- Noise reduction: minimize grain or digital noise
- Sharpening: enhance details but avoid over-sharpening
- Retouching: remove dust, scratches, minor imperfections without over-editing
- Establish consistent look and feel across all images for professional cohesion

## Technical setup

- Use tripod for steady camera and precise composition control
- Shoot in RAW format for flexibility during editing
- Ensure sharp focus; consider shallow depth of field to isolate subject
- Pay attention to detail and item condition

## Documentation and authentication

- Provide high-quality photos and detailed descriptions
- Include certification from reputable services (CGC for comics, Wata/PSA for toys, etc.)
- Set competitive prices based on market research
- Keep all documentation: certificates, service records, correspondence with authenticators
- Store certification safely as important for value documentation
