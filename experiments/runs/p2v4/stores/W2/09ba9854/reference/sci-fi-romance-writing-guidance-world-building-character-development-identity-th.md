World-Building: Establish vision and tone before building. Develop rich history with significant events and tech advancements. Define world rules (physics, technology) and maintain consistency. Create diverse, immersive environments reflecting culture and history. Consider technology implications on daily life and society. Balance detail with ambiguity. Ground sci-fi in universal human emotions and experiences. Develop unique language and slang for authenticity.

Character Development: Give clear, specific, measurable goals and motivations. Create multi-dimensional characters with contradictory traits, flaws, and strengths. Develop authentic backstories showing how past shapes personality. Make emotional struggles relatable even in extraordinary circumstances. Create conflicts stemming from character motivations. Show emotional vulnerability and willingness to open up. Develop emotional intelligence. Make relationships authentic based on mutual respect and trust.

Character Arcs and Relationships: Show emotional growth, change, or perspective shift. Create character-driven plots where plot stems from character desires. Build romantic relationships on mutual respect, trust, and communication. Create tension from differences in values, goals, or past experiences. Show emotional intimacy and how characters support each other. Ensure two-way partnerships with agency for both characters. Avoid insta-love by building relationships gradually with focus on emotional intimacy.

Identity and Self-Discovery: Characters question their purpose and identity in extraordinary circumstances. Sci-fi elements blur boundaries between human/non-human, self/others, and reality/fantasy. Explore how characters form and reform identity in response to new experiences. Incorporate intersectionality (species, culture, gender, sexuality). Use inner conflict and trials by fire to reveal growth and strengths. Use character arcs, world-building, plot twists, and symbolism to explore identity themes throughout narrative.
