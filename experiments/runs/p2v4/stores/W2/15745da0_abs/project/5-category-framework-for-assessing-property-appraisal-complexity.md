Checklist categories for determining whether an appraisal order qualifies as complex and may justify higher fees:

1. **Location** — Remote/limited access areas, unique environmental features (hillside, water, forest), crime/hazard zones, unusual street layout (cul-de-sac, steep driveway)

2. **Property type and design** — Unusual/complex design (multi-unit, multiple levels, attached garage), special features (pool, hot tub, large deck), complex systems (geothermal, radiant heat), outbuildings (barn, shed, guesthouse)

3. **Age and condition** — Significantly older/newer than comparable, extensive repairs/renovations needed, significant wear (water damage, pest infestation), major structural alterations or conversions

4. **Zoning and land use** — Unique zoning restrictions (floodplain, conservation area, historic district), complex regulations (HOA rules, wetlands), zoning variances or conditional use permits

5. **Market conditions** — Rapid appreciation/decline, unique economic characteristics (vacation homes, high-end properties), unusual trends (unemployment, foreclosures), supply/demand factors (limited inventory, demand for specific features)

**Purpose:** Enable team to consistently and objectively assess property complexity and justify appraisal fee levels based on objective criteria.
