**Charles Dudley Arnold** (1865-1946) was an American photographer known for pioneering work in aerial and nighttime photography.

**Early career:**
- Born in New York City
- Studied at Art Students League of New York
- Began as portrait photographer
- Became interested in capturing images from unusual perspectives

**Aerial photography (1890s+):**
- One of the first photographers to experiment with aerial photography
- Mounted cameras on balloons in 1890s
- Early 1900s: began using airplanes as photography platforms
- Worked with pioneering aviators including Orville Wright
- Photographs characterized by innovative composition, capturing patterns and textures of landscape from new perspectives

**Nighttime photography:**
- Innovative work in an era when photography was primarily daytime
- Used artificial light sources: electric lights, gas lamps, torches
- Developed special equipment: flash powder, magnesium wire for bright light bursts
- Often photographed urban scenes (city streets, buildings)
- Characterized by dramatic contrasts between light and dark, capturing mystery and drama of night

**Legacy:** Established aerial photography as new genre; influenced photographers to experiment with similar techniques; recognized as important historical documents and works of art; continues to inspire photographers worldwide.
