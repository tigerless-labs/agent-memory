**Francis Benjamin Johnston** (1864-1952) was an American photographer and one of the first women to establish a professional photography career in the United States.

**Early career:**
- Born into prominent Virginia family
- Self-trained through apprenticeships with various photographers
- Opened studio in Washington, D.C. in 1894
- Quickly established reputation as talented and innovative photographer

**Major work:**

*Portraiture:* Commissioned portraits of prominent figures including:
- Theodore Roosevelt
- Mark Twain
- Susan B. Anthony
- Alice Roosevelt Longworth
- Other politicians, celebrities, and socialites
- Characterized by elegance, simplicity, and attention to detail

*Architectural photography:* 
- Pioneering work documenting America's historic buildings
- Traveled extensively throughout the country
- Focused on colonial-era and other historic structures
- Helped raise awareness of importance of preserving American architectural heritage

**Legacy:** Technical excellence, artistic innovation; committed to photography as a tool for social change; enduring influence on American photography; pioneer of women's participation in professional photography.
