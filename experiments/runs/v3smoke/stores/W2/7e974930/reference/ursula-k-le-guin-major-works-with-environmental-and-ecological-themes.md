Key works by Le Guin exploring environmental and social themes:
- **The Dispossessed** (1974) — anarchist commune on distant planet; explores anarchism as political system and human-environment relationship
- **The Left Hand of Darkness** (1969) — genderless inhabitants; explores gender, sexuality, power, and human-environment relationship
- **The Word for World is Forest** (1976) — conflict between human colonizers and native inhabitants; explores imperialism, colonialism, and environmental issues
- **The Lathe of Heaven** (1971) — man whose dreams change reality; critiques destructive human impact on environment and misuse of science/technology
- **Always Coming Home** (1985) — post-apocalyptic California; explores environmentalism, ecology, and human-nature relationship through stories, songs, poetry

Le Guin known for creating compelling stories exploring complex social/political issues, critiquing capitalist ideologies and destructive human impact on environment.
