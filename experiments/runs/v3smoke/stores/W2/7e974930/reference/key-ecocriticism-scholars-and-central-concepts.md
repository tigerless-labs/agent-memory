**Leading ecocriticism figures:**
- **Lawrence Buell** (American literary critic) — coined 'environmental imagination' (capacity of literature to shape how we think about environment and relate to it)
- **Cheryll Glotfelty** (American literary critic) — emphasizes ecocriticism's focus on representation of human-environment relationship in literature
- **Jonathan Bate** (British literary critic) — 'ecocriticism is not just reading nature into literature, but reading literature as aspect of relations between culture and nature'
- **Eric D. Wilson** (American literary critic) — emphasizes study of literature and environment as study of human culture and natural world

**Core ecocritical concepts:**
- Ecocriticism examines representation, shapes, and challenges perceptions of the natural world
- Considers environmental impact of literary production and consumption
- Uses close reading, historical/cultural context analysis, environmental science analysis, comparative analysis, ethnographic fieldwork, and discourse analysis
- Can be both interdisciplinary (combining disciplines to study problem) and transdisciplinary (involving stakeholders to solve real-world problems)
