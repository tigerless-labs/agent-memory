**Judy Chicago** (born Judith Sylvia Cohen, 1939) - legendary figure in feminist art movement.

**Education:** University of Illinois, Art Institute of Chicago, UCLA (MFA 1964)

**Major Works:**

*The Dinner Party (1974-1979)* - Most famous work. Monumental installation celebrating achievements of 39 women from history and mythology. Triangular table with 39 place settings featuring ceramic plates, utensils, napkins. Plates decorated with vulva-like forms symbolizing female sexuality and creativity. Now in permanent collection at Brooklyn Museum. Iconic symbol of feminist art.

*Atmospheric Sculptures (1960s-1970s)* - Environmental installations using colored smoke and lights for immersive experiences

*Birth Project (1980-1985)* - Paintings, weavings, sculptures exploring birth and motherhood themes

*PowerPlay (1982-1987)* - Paintings and drawings examining power dynamics and relationships

**Significance:** Key figure in 1970s feminist art movement. Addressed female sexuality, reproductive rights, erasure of women's contributions to history. Paved way for future feminist artists.

**Teaching & Activism:** Taught at CalArts, UCLA, Duke University. Advocate for women's rights, LGBTQ+ rights, environmental issues.

**Legacy:** Inspired artists including Faith Ringgold, Miriam Schapiro, Carolee Schneemann. Awarded National Medal of Arts (2018) - highest US government honor for artists.
