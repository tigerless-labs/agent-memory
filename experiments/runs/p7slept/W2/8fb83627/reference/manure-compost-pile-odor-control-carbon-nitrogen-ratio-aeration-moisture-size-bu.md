Carbon-to-Nitrogen ratio: Aim for 2/3 brown materials (straw, hay, shredded paper) to 1/3 green materials (manure). This balance reduces ammonia production.

Aeration: Regularly turn the compost pile to introduce oxygen and prevent anaerobic conditions that cause odors.

Moisture: Maintain consistent moisture level like a damp sponge. Too much moisture creates anaerobic conditions; too little slows decomposition.

Size and shape: Keep pile compact, around 3-4 feet diameter and 3-5 feet tall for efficient heat generation. Larger piles develop anaerobic pockets.

Bulking agents: Add straw, hay, or shredded newspaper to absorb excess moisture and provide structure.

Covering: Cover with carbon-rich material like straw or tarp to reduce odors and retain heat.

Location: Position downwind from living areas and away from water sources.

Temperature: Monitor for 130-140°F range. High temperatures kill pathogens and weed seeds, reducing odors.

Odor-reducing materials: Incorporate lime, dolomitic limestone, or zeolite to neutralize ammonia.
