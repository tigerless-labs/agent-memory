As of 2021, approximately 3,000 languages are classified as endangered.

**Classification Criteria:**
- Number of speakers (low population = risk)
- Age of speakers (predominantly elderly = risk)
- Intergenerational transmission (not passing to children = risk)
- Language prestige (devalued by dominant speakers = risk)
- Political/economic factors (war, displacement, globalization)

**Documentation & Preservation Methods:**
1. Language documentation (recordings, dictionaries, grammars, transcriptions)
2. Language revitalization programs (teaching, daily use, cultural pride)
3. Digital preservation (archives, apps, online resources)
4. Language immersion schools
5. Community-driven efforts

**Key Challenges:**
- Limited resources (time, funding, expertise)
- Lack of written materials
- Social/cultural barriers
- Language attrition
- Cultural misappropriation concerns
- Technical complexity

**Benefits:**
- Maintains linguistic and cultural diversity
- Preserves traditional ecological knowledge (climate change solutions)
- Strengthens community identity
- Cognitive benefits from multilingualism (problem solving, creativity, flexibility)
