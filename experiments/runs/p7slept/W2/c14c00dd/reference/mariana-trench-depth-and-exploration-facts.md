The Mariana Trench is approximately 36,070 feet (10,994 meters) deep in the western Pacific Ocean. Key exploration milestones: the Trieste reached the bottom in 1960. Scientists use deep-sea submersibles and unmanned underwater vehicles (UUVs) to explore and collect samples.

Notable species discovered: Mariana snailfish, amphipods, cusk eels. These creatures have adapted to extreme pressure and darkness, with adaptations including bioluminescence.

Approximately 90% of the ocean remains unexplored. Potential research applications include medical imaging, drug discovery, disease treatments, and construction materials from organisms adapted to extreme pressure.
