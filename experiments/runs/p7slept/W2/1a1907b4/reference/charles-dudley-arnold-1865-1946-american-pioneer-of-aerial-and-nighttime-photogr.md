American photographer known for pioneering work in aerial and nighttime photography.

**Life & Career:**
- Born 1865 in New York City; died 1946
- Studied photography at Art Students League of New York
- Began career as portrait photographer

**Aerial Photography Innovations:**
- One of first photographers to experiment with cameras mounted on balloons and airplanes
- In 1890s, began experimenting with aerial photography using balloon-mounted cameras
- In early 1900s, began using airplanes as photography platform—one of the first photographers to do so
- Worked with pioneering aviators, including Orville Wright
- Used variety of cameras and techniques to capture aerial images
- Photos characterized by innovative composition and ability to capture landscape patterns and textures from unique perspective

**Nighttime Photography:**
- Pioneering work in nighttime photography (unusual for the era)
- Used artificial light sources: electric lights, gas lamps, torches
- Developed special equipment including flash powder and magnesium wire
- Photographed urban scenes (city streets, buildings) with dramatic, moody, atmospheric effects
- Work characterized by sharp contrasts between light and dark, capturing mystery and drama of night

**Legacy:**
- His work established aerial photography as new and important genre
- Inspired other photographers to experiment with similar techniques
- Photographs recognized as important historical documents and works of art
- Continue to inspire and influence photographers worldwide
