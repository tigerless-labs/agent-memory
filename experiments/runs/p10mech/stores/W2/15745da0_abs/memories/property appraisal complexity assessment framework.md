## Purpose

Establish clear, objective guidelines for determining whether a property appraisal is complex and justifies higher fees. Helps the team consistently assess property characteristics and make well-informed fee decisions.

## Complexity Criteria Checklist

Key categories for evaluation:

- **Location**: Remote/limited access, unique geography (hillside, waterfront, dense forest), crime/hazard risks, challenging layouts (cul-de-sac, steep driveway)
- **Property type & design**: Unusual complexity (multi-unit, multiple levels, attached garage), special features (pool, hot tub, large deck), complex systems (geothermal, radiant heat), outbuildings (barn, shed, guesthouse)
- **Age & condition**: Significantly older/newer than area norm, requires extensive repairs, significant wear/damage (water damage, pest infestation), major alterations (conversion, additions)
- **Zoning & land use**: Unique restrictions (floodplain, conservation area, historic district), complex regulations (HOA rules, wetlands), variances or conditional use permits
- **Market conditions**: Rapidly appreciating/declining markets, unique demographics (vacation homes, high-end clusters), unusual trends (unemployment, foreclosures), supply/demand challenges

## Professional Advisory on Appraiser Bias

**Final approved version (2023-05-20):**

"It is essential to keep in mind that an appraiser's assessment of a property as complex does not conclusively establish its complexity. Although appraisers possess expertise and experience in evaluating the complexities of properties, it is imperative to meticulously consider their opinion and accompanying evidence in conjunction with our own research and analysis. This is especially important to remember as appraisers may have a vested interest in securing the highest fee possible, and may therefore be inclined to search for justifications of complexity. As a team, it is crucial that we exercise caution in our judgement and ensure that our decisions are informed by objective criteria and a comprehensive examination of the property and market conditions."

Key principle: Objective criteria and independent analysis must prevail over appraiser opinions, particularly given appraiser financial incentives.
