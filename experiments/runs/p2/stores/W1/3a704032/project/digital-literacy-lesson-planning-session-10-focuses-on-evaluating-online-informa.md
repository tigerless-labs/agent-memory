**Date started:** May 2023

**Project:** Creating a digital literacy curriculum series for 6th-grade elementary school students.

**Session 10 details (completed):**
- **Title:** Evaluating Online Information
- **Objective:** Students learn how to evaluate credibility and reliability of online sources

**Core teaching method:** CRAAP Test
- **C**urrency: When was information published/updated?
- **R**elevance: Is it relevant to the topic?
- **A**uthority: Who's the author? What are their credentials?
- **A**ccuracy: Is the information verifiable? Supported by evidence?
- **P**urpose: Why does this information exist? What's the agenda?

**Lesson structure:**
1. Introduction (10 min): Discuss importance of evaluating online sources
2. Instruction (20 min): Review CRAAP Test with examples (news articles, academic papers), have students practice on sample article
3. Activity (30 min): Each student finds 2 sources on assigned topic, applies CRAAP Test, records findings, presents to class explaining reasoning
4. Break (10 min)
5. Discussion (20 min): Create collaborative list of evaluation tips (checking author credentials, seeking alternative sources), discuss application
6. Conclusion (10 min): Recap importance, encourage use in future research

**Assessment methods:**
- Review student worksheets for accuracy/completeness
- Evaluate understanding via short quiz or class discussion
- Monitor group participation

**Educational tech tools considered for reflection/discussion time:**
- Padlet (collaborative virtual wall)
- Nearpod (interactive platform with polls and open-ended questions)
- Flipgrid (video discussion platform)
- Google Jamboard (virtual whiteboard for visual collaboration)

**Effective discussion facilitation methods:**
- Ask open-ended questions requiring more than yes/no
- Paraphrase/summarize student contributions to show value
- Encourage active listening between peers
- Use visual aids and graphic organizers
- Emphasize that all thoughts are valued
