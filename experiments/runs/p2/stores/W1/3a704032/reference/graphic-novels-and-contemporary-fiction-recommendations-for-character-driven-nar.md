**Recommendations given May 2023**

**Graphic novels similar to Saga by Brian K. Vaughan & Fiona Staples:**
- **Monstress** by Marjorie Liu & Sana Takeda — fantasy epic, young woman with supernatural abilities, stunning art, complex characters, moral ambiguity
- **The Wicked + The Divine** by Kieron Gillen & Jamie McKelvie — mythology + pop culture, gods reincarnate as pop stars, character-driven
- **Black Hammer** by Jeff Lemire & Dean Ormston — superhero deconstruction, heroes on rural farm after cosmic event, explores humanity of superheroes
- **The Umbrella Academy** by Gerard Way & Gabriel Bá — dysfunctional superhero family, action + drama + humor
- **Descender** by Jeff Lemire & Dustin Nguyen — sci-fi epic, young robot boy hunted by humans, explores identity and humanity
- **The Goddamned** by Jason Aaron & R.M. Guéra — biblical/mythological figures in dark reality, action + drama + horror
- **Y: The Last Man** by Brian K. Vaughan & Pia Guerra — last human male on Earth, explores gender and power (another Vaughan work)

**Contemporary fiction with strong female protagonists and complex relationships:**
- **The Devil Wears Prada** by Lauren Weisberger — demanding boss/assistant dynamic, female-focused relationships
- **The Mothers** by Brit Bennett — coming-of-age in tight-knit Southern California community, motherhood and identity themes
- **The Girls** by Emma Cline — 1960s, teenage girls in Manson-esque cult, female relationships and power dynamics
- **The Interestings** by Meg Wolitzer — group of friends from summer arts camp, lifelong complex relationships
- **Conversations with Friends** by Sally Rooney — young women, complex entanglement with a couple, power and desire
- **The Female Persuasion** by Meg Wolitzer — young woman's mentorship with famous feminist, identity and power themes
- **The Last Romantics** by Tara Conklin — four siblings, complex family relationships over years
- **The Age of Light** by Whitney Scharer — based on Lee Miller (model/photographer), Parisian avant-garde 1920s–1930s, relationships with men and artistic identity
