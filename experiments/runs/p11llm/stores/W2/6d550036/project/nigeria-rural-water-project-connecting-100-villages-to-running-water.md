## Project Overview
Planned infrastructure project in Nigeria to connect 100 rural villages to running water access.

## Stakeholder Analysis Framework
Developed comprehensive stakeholder analysis with 13 stakeholder groups categorized by benefit or harm.

### Beneficiary Stakeholders (8)
1. **Rural communities in 100 villages** (Individuals) – Improved access to clean water
2. **Local government and authorities** (Government) – Improved public health, reduced resource burden
3. **Water and sanitation organizations** (Non-profit) – Expanded reach, credibility improvement
4. **Health care providers and facilities** (Business/Non-profit) – Reduced illness cases, improved revenue
5. **Agricultural businesses and farmers** (Business) – Enhanced water access for irrigation
6. **Women and children** (Individuals) – Reduced collection time, more education/employment opportunity
7. **Schools and educational institutions** (Non-profit) – Water access for students and staff
8. **Local businesses and industries** (Business) – Production water access

### Potentially Harmed Stakeholders (5)
1. **Landowners and residents facing displacement** (Individuals) – Property loss, relocation
2. **Traditional water sources** (N/A) – Contamination or obsolescence
3. **Environmental organizations** (Non-profit) – Potential ecosystem impacts
4. **Local communities relying on traditional sources** (Individuals) – Loss of cultural/religious practice
5. **Small-scale water vendors** (Business) – Revenue loss from reduced demand

## Impact Indicators
Each stakeholder group has 2-3 measurable indicators: access rates, health outcomes, economic effects, time savings, and cultural/social factors.
