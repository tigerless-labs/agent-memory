Straight photography emerged in the 1920s-1930s as a style characterized by straightforward, objective approaches without artistic manipulation or effects.

**Origins & Philosophy:**
- Term coined by photographer and critic Alfred Stieglitz
- Rejected use of artificial effects or manipulation
- Sought to capture the world as it really was, emphasizing honesty and truthfulness
- Influenced by both Pictorialism and documentary traditions

**Key Photographers:**
- Edward Weston
- Paul Strand
- Ansel Adams

**Characteristics:**
- Simple, everyday subjects (landscapes, still lifes, portraits)
- Clarity, precision, and attention to detail
- Commitment to objectivity and the power of the photographic image
- Approach treated photography as legitimate art form in its own right

**Legacy:**
Continues to influence street photography, landscape photography, and portrait photography today.
