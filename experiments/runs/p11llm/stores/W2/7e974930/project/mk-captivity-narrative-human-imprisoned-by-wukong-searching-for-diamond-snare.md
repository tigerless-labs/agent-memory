## Story: MK and Wukong

### Setup
MK is trapped behind a magic barrier by Wukong, the Monkey King, who believes MK knows the location of "the diamond snare." MK insists he only knows old stories about it, not its actual location.

### Plot Arc Established
1. **Interrogation & negotiation**: Wukong attempts to torture the information from MK; MK eventually convinces Wukong to keep him alive "for now" as insurance against future need
2. **First sustenance**: A lesser monkey brings MK minimal food (fruit) and water without explanation
3. **Psychological toll**: MK struggles to reconcile the heroic Wukong of childhood legends with the cruel captor holding him prisoner—confused and scared
4. **Desperation during feast**: MK hears monkeys partying with a feast; desperate to escape and steal food, his attempts fail
5. **Negotiated provision**: MK begs for food/water using logic (Wukong can't use him as insurance if he starves). Wukong, annoyed by the pleas, eventually provides stale bread and water, warning him not to eat too fast

### Characters

**MK** (human)
- Weak physically, desperate to survive
- Attempts reasoning with Wukong despite terror
- Conflicted emotions: the legendary heroic Wukong from childhood stories vs. the cruel captor he experiences
- Shows desperation and will to live

**Wukong** (the Monkey King)
- Cruel and dismissive of humans as "pathetic," unable to defend themselves
- Goal-oriented: searching for the diamond snare
- Mercurial: willing to kill MK, then decides to keep him "for now," then reluctantly provides minimal sustenance
- Operates on self-interest (MK's value as insurance) rather than compassion
- Shows annoyance when pressured but yields when logic threatens his own objectives

### World Elements
- Magic barrier: strong enough to withstand repeated attacks
- Diamond snare: object Wukong seeks; purpose/nature unclear
- Monkey society: hierarchy with lesser monkeys serving Wukong; capable of feasts and celebrations
- Wukong's powers: lightning bolts, barrier control, super strength

### Themes
- Power imbalance and captivity
- Mythology meets brutal reality
- Survival through negotiation and psychological leverage
- Moral complexity: Wukong not purely evil—responds to self-interest logic
