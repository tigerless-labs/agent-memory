Worcester Royal Porcelain (1837-1901). Notable design styles: Japanese-inspired (1860s-1880s) with pale blue celadon glaze, cherry blossoms, chrysanthemums, birds; Aesthetic Movement (1870s-1890s) with botanical motifs; Renaissance Revival (1850s-1880s) with acanthus leaves and classical motifs; Moorcroft-style (1880s-1900s) with flambe glaze; Pomegranate and Floral Patterns; Gilt and Enamel Decoration; Enameled Vases with cloisonné and champlevé techniques. Notable artisans: Charles Baldwyn (1821-1898), George Owen (1845-1914), William Moorcroft (1855-1917), William Moore. Celadon glaze made with cobalt oxide and feldspathic base, fired at ~1200C, soft pale blue with natural and intentionally-enhanced crackle patterns. Identify by crown mark with Worcester name, date codes (dots/numbers), and artist signatures. Export markets included Europe (primary), North America, Asia (India and China treaty ports), and other regions; participated in major exhibitions like Great Exhibition of 1851.
