**Appraisal Process Steps:**

1. Search online for certified appraisers (look for NAJA, ASA, or AAA certification)
2. Ask friends and family for referrals to reputable appraisers
3. Get one appraisal per item initially; pursue multiple appraisals only if needed (high-value items, insurance requirements, or conflicting opinions)

**Priority Order:**
- Start with grandmother's diamond necklace (most valuable; likely to have insurance requirement first)
- Then antique music box (may need specialized appraiser experienced with mechanical/musical antiques)
- Then depression-era glassware (value depends on rarity and condition)

**After Appraisal:**
- Use appraisal report to shop for insurance companies specializing in high-value items
- Look for policies with agreed value coverage, low/no deductible, worldwide coverage, and mysterious disappearance protection
- Keep all appraisal reports and insurance policies organized and accessible

**Insurance Companies to Contact:**
Jewelry-focused: Jewelers Mutual, Chubb, AXA ART
General high-value: Hagerty, State Farm (specialized department), Allstate (Valuable Items policy), Liberty Mutual (Valuable Possessions policy)
Consider working with independent insurance brokers specializing in collectibles.
