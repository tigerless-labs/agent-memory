NGOs distributing aid in Africa face several interconnected challenges:

Infrastructure gaps: Lack of roads, bridges, ports makes transporting aid to remote areas difficult.

Corruption & mismanagement: Pervasive corruption means aid can be diverted or misused; manual systems lack proper audit trails.

High operational costs: Infrastructure limitations and need to import goods/services make daily operations expensive, limiting aid volume.

Political instability & conflict: Unsafe operating environments can restrict NGO activities.

Accountability gap: NGOs lack reliable, flexible systems to track and report on distribution activities transparently. Donors require accountability; communities need to trust the system. Manual data entry is error-prone and slow.

Communication barriers: Language and cultural differences complicate coordination with local communities.

Resource constraints: Limited funding, personnel, and equipment restrict NGO capacity.

Key insight: The lack of reliable accountability systems is central to many other challenges—it prevents transparency, enables corruption, and undermines donor and community trust.
