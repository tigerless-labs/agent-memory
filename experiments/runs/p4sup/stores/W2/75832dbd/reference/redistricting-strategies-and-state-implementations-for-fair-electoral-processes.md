## Strategies for Fair Redistricting

To ensure fair and unbiased redistricting processes:

1. **Independent redistricting commissions** — Nonpartisan commissions made up of citizens, selected through public application processes
2. **Transparent process** — Public input opportunities and clear, open criteria for drawing districts
3. **Objective criteria** — Focus on equal population distribution and Voting Rights Act adherence; avoid partisan criteria like voting history or party affiliation
4. **Judicial review** — Courts can evaluate maps for constitutionality and fairness, ordering redrawing if biased

## State Examples

**California (2010)**
- 14-member independent redistricting commission
- Members selected through public application process
- Must be impartial

**Arizona (2000)**
- 5-member independent redistricting commission
- Members selected through public application process
- Cannot be elected officials or political party officials

**Iowa**
- Nonpartisan redistricting for several decades
- Led by nonpartisan legislative services agency
- Uses population data and objective criteria
- Iowa legislature votes on proposals

**Colorado (2018)**
- 12-member independent redistricting commission
- Members selected by panel of retired judges
- Must be impartial
