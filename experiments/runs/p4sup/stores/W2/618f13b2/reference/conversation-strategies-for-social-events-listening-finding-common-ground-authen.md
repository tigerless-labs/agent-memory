## How to Approach Conversations at Events or Meetups\n\n### Foundation Principles\n1. **Be yourself** — authenticity is key; people sense insincerity\n2. **Listen actively** — show interest in what they're saying\n3. **Find common ground** — look for shared interests or experiences\n4. **Avoid dominating** — give the other person space to share\n5. **Use positive body language** — maintain eye contact, smile, open posture\n6. **Keep it brief at first** — don't feel pressured to have hour-long conversations\n\n### Conversation Flow\n- Start with a **friendly greeting** — "Hi, how's it going?"\n- Ask **open-ended questions** — encourage them to share more\n- **Listen more than you talk** — show genuine interest\n- **Exchange contact information** — if interested in staying in touch\n- **Follow up** — show you're genuinely interested in continuing the connection\n\n### Conversation Starters\n- "What brings you to this event?"\n- "How did you get into [shared interest]?"\n- "What do you think about [current event or popular topic]?"\n- "Have you tried [related activity or recommendation]?"\n- "What's been the highlight of your week?"\n\n### Reading the Room\n- Pay attention to body language and verbal cues\n- If someone seems uncomfortable or uninterested, politely excuse yourself\n- It's okay to move on — not every conversation will click
