## Methodology
Framework for measuring project impact across diverse stakeholder groups in infrastructure/development projects.

## Steps
1. **Identify all stakeholder groups** – Include both beneficiaries and potentially harmed parties
2. **Categorize stakeholders** – By type (individuals, government, business, non-profit, etc.)
3. **Describe benefit or harm** – Specific to each stakeholder's interaction with the project
4. **Define measurable indicators** – 2–3 quantifiable or observable indicators per stakeholder showing impact

## Indicator Categories
- **Access indicators** – Increase/decrease in service availability or usage rates
- **Health indicators** – Reduction/increase in illness cases or health outcomes  
- **Economic indicators** – Revenue changes, cost reductions, employment effects
- **Time/opportunity indicators** – Hours freed for other activities (education, work, etc.)
- **Social/cultural indicators** – Impact on practices, traditions, community satisfaction
- **Environmental indicators** – Quality changes, ecosystem effects

## Key Principle
Comprehensive view of project success requires measuring both benefit and harm across all affected parties—not just primary beneficiaries. Often-overlooked harms (displaced persons, traditional practice disruption, vendor livelihood loss) must be tracked to assess true net impact.
