Certain words in one language may not have exact equivalents in other languages, revealing differences in how cultures conceptualize and express meaning.\n\n**Example:**\n- **awkward** (English) — lacks precise equivalents in several languages\n\nThese gaps reflect cultural and social contexts unique to each language. When communicating across languages, these untranslatable nuances can:\n- Lead to miscommunications and misunderstandings\n- Create opportunities for creative language use (adapting words, using metaphors)\n- Require context and nonverbal cues to convey intended meaning\n\nThe existence of these gaps demonstrates that language is not a neutral tool for conveying pre-existing thoughts, but rather shapes what can be thought and expressed.
