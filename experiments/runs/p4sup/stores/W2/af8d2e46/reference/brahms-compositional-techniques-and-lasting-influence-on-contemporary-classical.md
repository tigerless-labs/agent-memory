## Brahms' Approach to Composition

**Traditional Techniques:**
- Used traditional forms: sonatas, variations, fugues
- Emphasized counterpoint and theme-and-variation development
- Valued balance, proportion, and unity
- Known for meticulous craftsmanship and attention to detail
- Careful orchestration with distinctive roles for each instrument

**Innovation Within Tradition:**
- Used unexpected chord progressions and rhythmic shifts to create tension
- Incorporated folk music elements from Germany and other regions
- Developed unique piano writing emphasizing both power and intimacy
- Experimented with harmonies and key relationships in his own way

**Influence on Contemporaries and Later Composers:**
- Direct influence: Antonín Dvořák (folk music, nationalistic elements), Gustav Mahler, Jean Sibelius
- Contemporary composers drawing from Brahms: Wolfgang Rihm, Thomas Adès, Brett Dean
- His emphasis on craftsmanship established a new standard for classical music refinement
- His work remains studied and performed today; considered enduringly influential rather than merely historical

**Keeping Audiences Engaged:**
- Performers bring interpretation and expression to traditional structures
- Listeners can focus on nuances like melody development and instrumental interplay
- Strategic use of surprise, contrast, and unpredictability within traditional frameworks
