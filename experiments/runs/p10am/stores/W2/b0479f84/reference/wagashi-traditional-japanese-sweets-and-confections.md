Wagashi refers to traditional Japanese sweets or confections often served at Japanese tea ceremonies or for special occasions. Known for beautiful presentation, delicate flavors, and intricate designs. Significant part of Japanese culture dating back to Heian period (794-1185 CE). Originally introduced from China and developed into unique Japanese art form reflecting aesthetic values of simplicity, elegance, and harmony with nature.\n\n**Types of Wagashi:**\n1. **Namagashi** - Fresh sweets made from seasonal ingredients, often served at tea ceremony\n2. **Higashi** - Dry sweets made from sugar, rice flour, or other ingredients, dried for preservation\n3. **Mochi** - Rice flour-based sweets, often filled with sweet fillings\n\n**Key Characteristics:**\n- Emphasizes use of seasonal ingredients to reflect changing seasons\n- Focuses on presentation, shape, and color aesthetics\n- Delicate, subtle flavors balancing sweetness with salt, umami, or acidity\n- Reflects Japanese aesthetic principles of simplicity and elegance\n\n**Examples:** Sakura mochi (cherry blossom-shaped), yokan, and other seasonal variations
