## Geological Composition

The Tennessee River watershed contains sedimentary rocks (limestone, sandstone, shale), metamorphic rocks (gneiss, schist), and igneous rocks (granite, diabase). Valley and ridge regions feature folded and faulted sedimentary rocks; Appalachian Mountains are primarily metamorphic rocks. Significant mineral deposits include coal, oil, and natural gas.

## Ecological Relationships

- Metamorphic rock areas support nutrient-rich soils with diverse plant species
- Limestone influences water pH and aquatic species abundance
- Rock formations create microhabitats for fish species
- Cumberland Plateau and Appalachian caves provide habitat for bats, birds, mammals

## Endangered Species

- Freshwater mussels: multiple endangered species, water-quality sensitive
- Fish: yellowfin madtom, pygmy madtom
- Critically dependent on clean water and stable river conditions

## Conservation Organizations

- Tennessee River Basin Network: multi-stakeholder collaboration (non-profits, government, business) focused on water quality, sustainable land use, habitat conservation
- Tennessee River Freshwater Pearl Mussel Conservation Fund: research and habitat restoration
- Tennessee Wildlife Federation: runs Tennessee's Wild Side project
- Tennessee Department of Environment and Conservation
- U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service

## Pollution Management

- Tennessee River Basin Initiative (TRBI): multi-state program for water quality monitoring, pollution source identification, management practices; has reduced sediment and nutrient pollution
- Clean Water Act: federal regulation of navigable waters
- Tennessee water quality standards: discharge limits on industries and wastewater treatment
- Ongoing concern: bioaccumulation in food chains, persistent pollution levels
