**Serving Etiquette:**
- Use small ceramic cups (ochoko or sakazuki)
- Pour for guests, not yourself (sign of hospitality)
- Guests receive with both hands (sign of respect)
- Never leave a cup empty
- Pour with right hand only (left hand considered unclean in traditional culture)

**Temperature & Storage:**
- Serve at room temperature or slightly warmed
- Some premium sakes best served chilled
- Store in refrigerator to preserve flavor and aroma

**Presentation:**
- Consider serving in traditional masu (wooden box) for festive touch

**Recommended Pairings:**
- Junmai: rich, full-bodied flavor with sushi and Japanese dishes
- Ginjo: light, crisp, with floral/fruity notes for delicate flavors like raw fish
- Daiginjo: high-end, complex, elegant for grilled/broiled dishes

**Types:**
- Junmai: premium sake with rich flavor profile
- Ginjo: light, crisp sake with floral and fruity notes
- Daiginjo: high-end sake with complex, elegant profile
