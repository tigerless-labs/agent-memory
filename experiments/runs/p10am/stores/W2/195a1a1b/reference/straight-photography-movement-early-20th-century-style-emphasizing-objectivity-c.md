Straight photography emerged in the 1920s-1930s as a style rejecting manipulation and artistic effects. The term was coined by Alfred Stieglitz, who believed photography should be treated as an art form emphasizing honesty and truthfulness. Key photographers included Edward Weston, Paul Strand, and Ansel Adams. They captured everyday subjects—landscapes, still lifes, portraits—with clarity and precision. The movement rejected Pictorialism's artificial effects while influenced by documentary tradition. Today its influence appears across street, landscape, and portrait photography.
