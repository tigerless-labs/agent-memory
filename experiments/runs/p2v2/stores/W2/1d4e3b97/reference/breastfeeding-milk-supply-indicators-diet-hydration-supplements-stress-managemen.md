## Signs Baby Is Getting Enough Milk
- Weight gain: 5–7 ounces per week
- Wet diapers: 6–8 per day
- Dirty diapers: 6–8 per week
- Feeding frequency: every 2–3 hours
- Behavior: content, appears satisfied or asleep after feeding

## Increasing Milk Supply
- Pump between feedings to increase stimulation frequency
- Pump in addition to breastfeeding, not as replacement
- Increase nursing session frequency
- Ensure proper latch (major factor)
- Adequate rest and stress management
- Consult lactation consultant if concerned

## Common Factors in Low Supply
- Infrequent breastfeeding/pumping
- Poor latch
- Stress and fatigue
- Health conditions: hypothyroidism, PCOS, previous breast surgery
- Some medications can affect milk production

## Diet & Hydration
- Dehydration can affect supply; drink to thirst
- Aim for pale yellow urine
- Balanced diet: fruits, vegetables, whole grains, proteins (meats, poultry, fish, nuts, legumes), healthy fats
- No specific foods must be avoided universally
- Some babies sensitive to: dairy, soy, wheat, nuts, caffeine (not all babies)

## Supplements Traditionally Used
- Fenugreek: herb, capsule or tea form
- Blessed thistle: herb, capsule or tea form
- Milk thistle: may support liver function and supply
- Brewer's yeast: contains B vitamins and minerals for production
- Fennel: herb, tea or supplement form
- **Important:** Consult healthcare provider/lactation consultant before taking; supplements not substitute for frequent effective breastfeeding/pumping

## Stress & Milk Quality
- Stress increases cortisol, which inhibits oxytocin (needed for letdown)
- Milk composition changes with stress: higher cortisol, lower lactose
- Occasional stress unlikely to significantly impact supply or quality
- Sustained stress warrants management strategies: exercise, adequate sleep, family support
