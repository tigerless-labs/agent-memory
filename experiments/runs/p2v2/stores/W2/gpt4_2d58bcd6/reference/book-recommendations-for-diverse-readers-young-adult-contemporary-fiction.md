Recommendations for readers interested in diverse literature, social justice, and character-driven narratives:\n\n**Similar to The Hate U Give:**\n- 'Dear Martin' by Nic Stone - Black teenager wrongly accused, writes letters to MLK\n- 'The Poet X' by Elizabeth Acevedo - Novel-in-verse about Afro-Latina teen, slam poetry, identity\n- 'Long Way Down' by Jason Reynolds - Teen dealing with brother's murder, cycle of violence\n- 'All American Boys' by Jason Reynolds & Brendan Kiely - Police brutality incident told from two perspectives\n- 'The Sun is Also a Star' by Nicola Yoon - Jamaican-American/Korean-American romance, identity themes\n\n**Similar to The Nightingale:**\n- Works by Renee Watson: 'Piecing Me Together', 'Betty Before X' - Focus on Black girls/women, identity, social justice\n\n**Similar to The Seven Husbands of Evelyn Hugo:**\n- Works by Nicola Yoon: Lyrical prose, complex characters, blend of realistic and speculative elements\n\n**Authors for eclectic tastes (blend genres, push boundaries):**\n- Jason Reynolds - Social justice, identity, Black experience in YA\n- Nicola Yoon - Identity, family, social justice themes\n- Renee Watson - Strong female protagonists, character-driven, social justice focus\n- Ibi Zoboi - 'American Street', 'Pride' - Haitian-American experiences, identity themes\n- Dhonielle Clayton - 'The Belles', 'Shattered Midnight' - Fantasy/speculative fiction\n- Tomi Adeyemi - 'Children of Blood and Bone' - African mythology, fantasy, social commentary\n- Sabaa Tahir - 'An Ember in the Ashes' - Roman-inspired world-building, fantasy, social commentary\n- Nnedi Okorafor - 'Who Fears Death', 'Binti' - African culture, mythology, fantasy
