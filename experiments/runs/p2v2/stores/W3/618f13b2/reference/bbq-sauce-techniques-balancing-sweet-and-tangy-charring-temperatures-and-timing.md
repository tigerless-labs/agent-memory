# BBQ Sauce - Sweet and Tangy Balance\n\n## Key Components\nGood BBQ sauce combines: sweet, tangy, smoky, and spicy flavors.\n\n## Sweetness Ingredients\n- Sugar, brown sugar, honey, molasses, maple syrup\n- Fruit-based: pineapple, apples\n- Starting ratio: 1:1 or 1:2 sweet to tangy\n\n## Tanginess Ingredients  \n- Vinegar: apple cider, balsamic, or white\n- Citrus juice: lemon, lime, or orange\n- Tomatoes\n- Start with smaller amounts - tanginess can quickly overpower\n\n## Balancing Tips\n1. Taste as you go and adjust\n2. Use a basic recipe as starting point, then tweak\n3. Consider BBQ style (Kansas City, Carolina, Texas) and meat type (beef, pork, chicken)\n4. Experiment with different sweet:tangy ratios (2:1, 3:1, 4:1)\n5. Be mindful of acidity level (citrus/vinegar)\n6. Add umami depth with: soy sauce, Worcestershire sauce, mushroom-based ingredients\n7. Let sauce sit hours or overnight to allow flavors to meld\n\n## General Tips\n- Use high-quality, fresh ingredients\n- Don't oversweeten or over-tangify\n- Start simple, gradually add ingredients\n- **Smoked paprika**: adds smoky depth without overwhelming other flavors - excellent addition to most BBQ sauces\n\n# Charring Techniques on BBQ/Grill\n\n## Temperature\n- **Direct grilling**: 350-400°F (175-200°C) for initial sear\n- **Finishing**: 225-250°F (110-120°C) to cook through without burning\n- Preheat grill 15-20 minutes before cooking\n\n## Timing\n- **Initial sear**: 2-3 minutes per side (depending on meat thickness)\n- **After sear**: Move to cooler part of grill to finish cooking\n- This creates crust while preventing burning\n\n## Technique\n1. Oil the grates before cooking (prevents sticking, promotes char)\n2. **Don't press down** on meat with spatula (squeezes out juices, prevents char)\n3. Rotate and flip regularly for even cooking\n4. Choose high-lignin wood for charcoal/wood fires: hickory, mesquite\n5. Keep grill clean and debris-free\n6. Patience - char development takes time\n\nSource: Discussed May 2023
