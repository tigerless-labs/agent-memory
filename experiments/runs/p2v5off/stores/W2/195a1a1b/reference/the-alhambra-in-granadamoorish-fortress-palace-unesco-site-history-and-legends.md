## Overview
Built in the 13th and 14th centuries during the Moorish reign in Granada, Spain. UNESCO World Heritage Site and one of Spain's most visited landmarks.

## Etymology & Architecture
- Name from Arabic "Al-Hamra" meaning "the red one" (referring to reddish walls and towers)
- Originally built as a fortress to defend against Christian attacks
- Used as royal palace by Nasrid dynasty; served as residence of sultan and court
- Center of political and cultural activity during Nasrid period

## Historical Timeline
- Conquered by Christian forces in 1492
- Used as royal palace by Catholic monarchs with additions made
- Fell into disrepair over centuries
- Restored in 19th and 20th centuries to current state

## Associated Legends & Mythology
- **Fountain of Lions**: Drinking from it supposedly makes you fall in love with Granada and never want to leave
- **Tower of the Seven Floors**: Said to be haunted by ghost of a princess imprisoned by her father for forbidden love with a Christian knight
- **Hall of Abencerrajes**: Believed to be site of massacre of the Abencerraje family; bloodstains reportedly visible on walls
- **Devil's Megaphone**: A spot in the Generalife palace gardens with perfect acoustics where the devil is supposedly heard speaking

## Other Granada Landmarks with Views
- **Mirador de San Nicolas**: Viewpoint on Albaicín hill with panoramic views of city, Alhambra, and Sierra Nevada Mountains
- **Sacromonte Abbey**: Hilltop monastery with panoramic views of Granada and surroundings
- **El Salvador Church**: Bell tower with bird's eye view of city center
- **Albaicín Quarter**: Narrow alleys and whitewashed buildings offering multiple vantage points
