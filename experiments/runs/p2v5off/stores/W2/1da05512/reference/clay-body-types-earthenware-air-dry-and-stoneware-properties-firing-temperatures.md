## Earthenware Clay\n**Strengths**: Easy to work with (soft and pliable), low firing temperature (~1800°F/980°C), wide range of colorful glaze options\n**Weaknesses**: Brittle and prone to cracking/breaking, not suitable for functional ware (not food-safe or durable enough for everyday use)\n\n## Air-Dry Clay\n**Strengths**: No kiln required, fast results (no firing process), easy to repair (can be re-moistened)\n**Weaknesses**: Limited durability (prone to cracking), not suitable for outdoor use, limited size and complexity (best for smaller, simpler projects)\n\n## Stoneware Clay\n**Strengths**: Strong and durable, high-temperature firing (~2400°F/1300°C), food-safe and watertight, resistant to thermal shock and wear, ideal for functional ware and outdoor use\n**Weaknesses**: More challenging to work with (dense, less pliable), requires high-temperature kiln, limited glaze options (needs specific high-temperature glazes)\n\n## Key Considerations\n- Choose food-safe or lead-free glazes for functional ware\n- Test glazes on non-functional pieces first\n- Keep detailed records of glaze tests, applications, and firing conditions
