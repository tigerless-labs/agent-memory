Key Statistics: As of 2021, approximately 3,000 languages classified as endangered.

Classification Criteria: (1) Number of remaining speakers — low count indicates extinction risk; (2) Age of speakers — predominantly elderly with few young speakers; (3) Intergenerational transmission — not passed to children indicates attrition; (4) Language prestige — lack of perceived value by dominant speakers; (5) Political/economic factors — war, displacement, globalization.

Preservation Methods: (1) Language documentation — audio/video recording, dictionaries, grammars, oral story transcription; (2) Revitalization programs — community teaching, daily use support, cultural pride; (3) Digital preservation — archives, mobile apps, online learning; (4) Immersion schools — child-centered with cultural activities; (5) Community-driven efforts — heritage and traditional practice maintenance.

Main Challenges: (1) Limited resources — time, funding, expertise often unavailable; (2) Lack of written materials; (3) Social/cultural factors — low prestige, limited perceived benefit, access barriers; (4) Language attrition — few native speakers, incomplete proficiency; (5) Cultural sensitivity — risk of misappropriation.

Arguments for Preservation: preserves unique heritage; maintains linguistic/cultural diversity essential for identity; preserves traditional ecological knowledge relevant to environmental challenges; provides cognitive benefits (problem-solving, creativity); strengthens marginalized community identity; value beyond economic utility.
