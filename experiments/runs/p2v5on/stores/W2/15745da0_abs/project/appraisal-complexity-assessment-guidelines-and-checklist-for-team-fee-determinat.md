Purpose: Guide team in objectively determining whether an appraisal order qualifies as complex and justifies higher fees.

Key Principle: An appraiser's assessment does not conclusively establish complexity. Team must rigorously evaluate opinions against independent research, recognizing appraisers have financial incentives to justify higher fees.

Complexity Checklist - Location: Remote areas, unique environmental features, crime rates, challenging street layouts

Complexity Checklist - Property type and design: Unusual designs, special features (pools/decks), specialized systems (geothermal/radiant heat), outbuildings

Complexity Checklist - Age and condition: Significantly older/newer properties, extensive repairs, unusual wear/damage, major alterations

Complexity Checklist - Zoning and land use: Floodplain restrictions, conservation areas, historic districts, HOA rules, wetlands

Complexity Checklist - Market conditions: Rapidly appreciating/declining markets, unique economic demographics, supply/demand factors

Team Decision Standard: Decisions must be informed by objective criteria and comprehensive property/market analysis, avoiding appraiser bias toward fee maximization.
