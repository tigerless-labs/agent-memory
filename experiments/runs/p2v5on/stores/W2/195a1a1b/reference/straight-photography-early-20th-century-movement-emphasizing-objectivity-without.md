Photography movement emerged in early 20th century (1920s-1930s), characterized by straightforward, objective approach without manipulation or artistic effects. Term coined by Alfred Stieglitz, who believed photography should be treated as art form in its own right while maintaining honesty and truthfulness. Influenced by Pictorialism and documentary tradition, but rejected artificial effects. Key photographers: Edward Weston, Paul Strand, Ansel Adams. Featured simple, everyday subjects (landscapes, still lifes, portraits) with clarity, precision, and attention to detail. Committed to capturing the world as it really was. Continues to influence modern photography genres.
