**Paper:** Detecting stochastic gravitational waves with binary resonance  
**Authors:** Diego Blas, Alexander C. Jenkins  
**Institutions:** Departament de Física, Universitat Autònoma de Barcelona; IFAE Barcelona; Theoretical Particle Physics and Cosmology Group, King's College London  
**Publication:** Phys. Rev. D (closed access)  
**Date published:** Feb 8, 2023  

**Key concept:** Uses binary systems to detect gravitational waves by exploiting resonance when gravitational wave frequency matches orbital frequency. Accumulation of oscillations over time makes tiny perturbations observable.

**Gap filled:** Microhertz region (10⁻⁶ Hz) — currently uncovered between pulsar timing arrays and space-based interferometers like LISA.

**Proposed application:** Earth-Moon system (orbital period ~28 days = 10⁶ seconds, frequency 10⁻⁶ Hz). Requires measuring Earth-Moon distance to ~0.1 mm accuracy using existing retroreflectors left by Apollo and Soviet missions.

**Technical basis:** Gravitational waves stretch and compress the separation of orbiting bodies. With proper resonance, effects accumulate and become measurable with current technology.
