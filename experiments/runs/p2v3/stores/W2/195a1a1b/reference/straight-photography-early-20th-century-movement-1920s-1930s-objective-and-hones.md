Photography movement that emerged in early 20th century, particularly 1920s-1930s.

**Core principles:**
- Straightforward and objective approach without manipulation or artistic effects
- Aimed to produce truthful, honest, objective representations of the world
- Rejected use of artificial effects or manipulation
- Sought to capture the world as it really was, without embellishments or distortions

**Origins & key figures:**
- Term coined by photographer and critic Alfred Stieglitz
- Stieglitz encouraged experimentation while emphasizing honesty and truthfulness
- Famous photographers in the movement: Edward Weston, Paul Strand, Ansel Adams

**Influences & scope:**
- Influenced by Pictorialism (artistic qualities) and documentary tradition (social/political focus)
- Distinguished by commitment to objectivity unlike its influences
- Often featured simple, everyday subjects: landscapes, still lifes, portraits
- Photographers approached subjects with clarity, precision, attention to detail

**Legacy:**
- Influence visible in modern street, landscape, and portrait photography
- Celebrated for commitment to objectivity, truthfulness, power of photographic image
- Continues to shape approach to photography and how it captures essence of the world
