## Cargo Loading Process
1. Preparation: establish secure area for unloading from supply vessel
2. Transportation: via helicopter, cargo planes, or boats depending on size and accessibility
3. Securing: ropes, chains, or straps to prevent damage
4. Lift: cranes or davits mounted on ship to bring cargo aboard using hoists/pulleys
5. Stowage: designated storage areas per cargo management plan and ship stability

## Cargo Unloading Process
1. Preparation: identify secure deck area for temporary storage
2. Retrieval: via cranes or cargo equipment from storage areas
3. Securing: attach to equipment, lift out of ship
4. Transportation: to final destination via helicopter, planes, or other transport

## Safety Measures
- Cargo properly secured to prevent damage from weather/movement
- Detailed cargo management plan developed
- Specialized protective gear (hardhats, gloves, goggles, heavy clothing)
- Clear crew communication essential
- Regular training, assessments, and emergency drills
- Continuous weather monitoring (wind, waves, visibility)
- High-standard navigation equipment for ice/hazard avoidance

## Crew Duration
- Typical expeditions: weeks to several months
- Rare cases: up to one year
- Crew works long rotating shifts
- Trained in survival skills, isolation coping, extreme weather adaptation
- Physical and mental well-being prioritized

## Challenges
- Unpredictable Arctic weather patterns
- Rough sea conditions
- Limited daylight hours in winter
- Potential icebergs and underwater hazards
