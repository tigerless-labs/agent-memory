## Classification Criteria
- Number of remaining speakers (low speaker count = high risk)
- Age of speakers (mostly elderly speakers = high risk)
- Intergenerational transmission (not passed to children = high risk)
- Language prestige (low status or replacement risk)
- Political and economic factors (war, displacement, globalization)

## Preservation Methods
- Language documentation: recording native speakers, creating dictionaries/grammars, transcribing oral traditions
- Language revitalization programs: teaching new learners, promoting daily use, building community pride
- Digital preservation: audio/video archives, mobile language apps, online learning resources
- Language immersion schools: supportive environment for children, cultural activities
- Community-driven efforts: heritage preservation, cultural practices, traditional knowledge

## Key Challenges
- Limited resources (time, funding, expertise)
- Lack of written materials
- Social and cultural factors (low perceived status)
- Language attrition (few native speakers, incomplete command of language)
- Cultural sensitivity (sacred practices/beliefs)
- Language-specific challenges (complex grammar, difficult transcription)

## Notable Facts
- As of 2021: approximately 3,000 languages classified as endangered
- Practical benefits include preserving traditional ecological knowledge relevant to environmental challenges
- Multilingualism has cognitive benefits: improved problem-solving, creativity, mental flexibility
