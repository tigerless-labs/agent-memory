**Biography:**\nBorn Judith Sylvia Cohen (1939) in Chicago, Illinois. Studied at University of Illinois, Art Institute of Chicago, UCLA (MFA 1964).\n\n**Major works:**\n- The Dinner Party (1974-1979) — monumental installation celebrating 39 women from history and mythology. Triangular table with 39 place settings featuring ceramic plates with vulva-like forms symbolizing female sexuality and creativity. Now in Brooklyn Museum permanent collection.\n- Atmospheric Sculptures (1960s-1970s) — environmental installations using colored smoke and lights\n- Birth Project (1980-1985) — paintings, weavings, sculptures exploring birth and motherhood\n- PowerPlay (1982-1987) — paintings and drawings examining power dynamics and relationships\n\n**Significance:**\nKey figure in 1970s feminist art movement challenging patriarchal structures of art world. Addressed female sexuality, reproductive rights, and erasure of women's contributions. Influenced artists like Faith Ringgold, Miriam Schapiro, Carolee Schneemann.\n\n**Teaching and activism:**\nTaught at CalArts, UCLA, Duke University. Advocate for women's rights, LGBTQ+ rights, environmental issues.\n\n**Recognition:**\nAwarded National Medal of Arts (2018), the highest U.S. government honor for artists.
