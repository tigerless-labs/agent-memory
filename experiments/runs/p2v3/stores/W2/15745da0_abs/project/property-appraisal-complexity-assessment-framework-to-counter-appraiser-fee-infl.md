Built a multi-criteria checklist to objectively assess whether a property appraisal is genuinely complex, to counter the bias that appraisers have financial incentive to overstate complexity to justify higher fees.

**Purpose:** Enable the team to consistently and objectively determine which appraisals justify higher fees based on actual property complexity rather than appraiser preference.

**Key criteria in the checklist:**
- Location (remote access, environmental features, hazards, street layout)
- Property type and design (multi-unit, multiple levels, special features like pools, unusual HVAC systems, outbuildings)
- Age and condition (age relative to area, repair needs, wear patterns, structural alterations)
- Zoning and land use (floodplain, conservation area, historic district, wetlands, HOA restrictions)
- Market conditions (appreciation/decline trends, economic demographics, supply/demand factors)

**Critical insight:** An appraiser's opinion that a property is complex should not be taken at face value. The team must rigorously weigh appraiser opinion against independent analysis and objective criteria, because appraisers have a vested interest in claiming complexity to collect higher fees.
