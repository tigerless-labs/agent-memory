## How the Fed Works
- FOMC (Federal Open Market Committee) meets 8 times per year
- Fed adjusts federal funds rate (overnight lending rate between banks)
- Lower rates: cheaper borrowing → stimulus; higher rates: expensive borrowing → inflation control
- Fed's mandate: stable prices, maximum employment, moderate long-term interest rates

## Historical Crisis Examples
- Great Depression (1929-1933): Fed initially raised rates, later adopted expansionary policy
- 1970s-80s inflation: Used reserve requirement adjustments and open market operations
- 2007-2009 Financial Crisis: Created lending facilities, purchased securities, near-zero rates
- COVID-19 (2020): Interest rate cuts, lending facilities, security purchases

## Personal Finance Impact
- Mortgages: Rate changes affect home affordability
- Credit cards: Impact borrowing costs
- Savings accounts: Affect returns on deposits
- Inflation: Higher rates reduce excess borrowing; lower rates can increase inflation

## Staying Informed
1. Follow financial news (websites, broadcasts, podcasts)
2. Check with bank/financial institution directly
3. Monitor mortgage rates if relevant
4. Consider consulting a financial advisor
