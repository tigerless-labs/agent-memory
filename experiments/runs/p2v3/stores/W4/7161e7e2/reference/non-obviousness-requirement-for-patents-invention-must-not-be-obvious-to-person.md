## Definition
Non-obviousness is a patent requirement stating that an invention must NOT be obvious to a person having ordinary skill in the relevant technology field.

## Examples

### Solar Panel Example
- **Obvious** (fails patentability): If a skilled solar technologist could easily create the same panel using known techniques and existing technologies
- **Non-obvious** (qualifies for patent): If inventor uses completely new approach—novel material or new manufacturing method—resulting in something not derivable from existing knowledge

### Water Purification Example
- **Obvious** (fails patentability): If a water purification expert could easily come up with the process using known techniques and technologies
- **Non-obvious** (qualifies for patent): If inventor develops new way of purifying water using a specific type of bacteria in an innovative manner

## Summary
Non-obviousness requires that the invention:
- Be something new and innovative
- Not be easily derivable from simple experimentation or modification of existing processes/products
- Represent a genuine advance beyond what skilled practitioners would ordinarily conceive
