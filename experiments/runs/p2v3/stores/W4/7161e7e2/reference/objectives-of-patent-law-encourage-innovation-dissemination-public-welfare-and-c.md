Patent law provides inventors temporary monopoly on use and commercial exploitation of inventions, with these objectives:

1. **Encourage innovation**: Incentivizes inventors to invest time and resources in R&D by granting exclusive profit rights for a set period

2. **Disseminate new technologies**: Encourages public disclosure of inventions through patent application, allowing others to learn from and build upon innovations

3. **Promote public welfare**: Grants patents only for new, useful, non-obvious inventions, ensuring socially beneficial technologies receive monopoly protection

4. **Balance public and private interest**: Allows others to use invention after patent expires, balancing inventor rights with societal needs

5. **Encourage foreign investment**: Protects foreign companies' patents in India while helping Indian companies protect patents globally

6. **Protect small and medium enterprises**: Provides equal protection for SMEs as large companies, promoting innovation and market competition

7. **Promote competition**: Encourages multiple companies to develop competing technologies and prevents single-company market domination through exclusive technology use
