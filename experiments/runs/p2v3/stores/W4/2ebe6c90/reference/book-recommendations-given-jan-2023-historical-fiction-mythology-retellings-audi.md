## Historical Fiction with Strong Female Leads\n- The Alice Network by Kate Quinn (WWI/WWII female spies)\n- The Red Tent by Anita Diamant (biblical retelling)\n- The Girl with the Pearl Earring by Tracy Chevalier\n- The Other Boleyn Girl by Philippa Gregory\n- The Paris Wife by Paula McLain (Ernest Hemingway's first wife)\n- The Women in the Castle by Jessica Shattuck (WWII Germany)\n- The Guernsey Literary and Potato Peel Pie Society by Mary Ann Shaffer\n- The Heretic's Daughter by Kathleen Kent (witch trials)\n- The Lost Queen by Signe Pike (6th-century Scottish queen)\n\n## Audiobooks with Feminist/Dystopian Themes\n- The Handmaid's Tale by Margaret Atwood (narr. Claire Danes)\n- The Testaments by Margaret Atwood (narr. Ann Dowd, Bryce Dallas Howard, Mae Whitman)\n- The Girls by Emma Cline (narr. Cady McClain)\n- The Belles by Dhonielle Clayton (narr. Rosie Jones)\n- The Fifth Season by N.K. Jemisin (narr. Robin Miles)\n- The Disasters by M.K. England (narr. Bahni Turpin)\n- The Outside by Ada Hoffmann (narr. January LaVoy)\n\n## Mythology/Folklore Retellings (Non-Western)\n- Anansi Boys by Neil Gaiman (African)\n- The Black Tides of Heaven by J.Y. Yang (Chinese)\n- The Tiger's Daughter by K. Arsenault Rivera (Japanese/Mongolian)\n- The Fisherman by John Langan (West African)\n- The City of Brass by S.A. Chakraborty (Middle Eastern, 18th-century Cairo)\n- The House of Shattered Wings by Aliette de Bodard (Vietnamese)\n- The Bone Shard War by Andrea Stewart (Chinese/Southeast Asian)\n- The Obsidian Tower by Melissa Caruso (Latin American/Mesoamerican)\n- The House of Whispers by Laura Purcell (African/Caribbean)\n- The Deep by Rivers Solomon (African diasporic)\n\n## Other Mythology/Folklore\n- The Penelopiad by Margaret Atwood (Greek, Penelope's perspective)\n- The Gilded Wolves by Roshani Chokshi (Indian/French)\n- The Library of the Unwritten by A.J. Hackwith (multicultural)\n- The Bear and the Nightingale by Katherine Arden (Russian)\n- The Silence of the Girls by Pat Barker (Greek, Briseis's perspective)
