Breastfeeding Milk Supply Guide

Signs Baby Is Getting Enough Milk:
- Weight gain: 5-7 ounces per week
- Diapers: 6-8 wet diapers daily; 6-8 dirty diapers weekly
- Feeding patterns: Every 2-3 hours, appears satisfied
- Behavior: Content, relaxed after feeding, may fall asleep

Pumping Between Feedings:
- Can increase milk supply by stimulating more frequent production
- Should supplement breastfeeding, not replace it
- Consult lactation consultant before starting a regimen

Common Causes of Low Milk Supply:
1. Infrequent breastfeeding/pumping — regular emptying is essential
2. Poor latch — lactation consultant can assess and correct
3. Stress and fatigue — impacts production; rest and stress management help
4. Health conditions — hypothyroidism, PCOS, previous breast surgery
5. Medications — some affect milk production

Hydration:
- Important for overall health and preventing dehydration
- Doesn't directly increase supply on its own
- Drink to satisfy thirst; pale yellow urine is a good indicator
- Milk, juice, soup, and high-water foods all contribute

Diet:
- No specific foods must be avoided universally
- Common triggers for some babies: dairy, soy, wheat, nuts, caffeine (but not universal)
- Balanced diet should include fruits, vegetables, whole grains, protein, healthy fats

Supplements That May Help:
- Fenugreek, blessed thistle, milk thistle, brewer's yeast, fennel
- All available in capsule, powder, or tea form
- Consult healthcare provider before taking to check for interactions

Stress and Milk Quality:
- High stress increases cortisol, which inhibits oxytocin (let-down reflex)
- Can change milk composition: higher cortisol, lower lactose
- Occasional stress unlikely to significantly impact supply
- Regular self-care helps reduce stress
