Straight photography is a photographic style that emerged in the 1920s-1930s, characterized by straightforward, objective representation without manipulation or artistic effects. The term was coined by Alfred Stieglitz, who believed photography should be treated as an art form while maintaining honesty and truthfulness. Key photographers: Edward Weston, Paul Strand, Ansel Adams. Rejected artificial effects in favor of capturing the world as it really was.
