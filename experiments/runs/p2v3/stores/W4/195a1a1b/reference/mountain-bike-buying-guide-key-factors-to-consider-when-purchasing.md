Key factors when buying a mountain bike:\n\n**Budget:** Set a price range; bikes range from hundreds to thousands of dollars.\n\n**Wheel Size:**\n- 26" (older standard, good for tight trails)\n- 27.5" (versatile, all-around)\n- 29" (fast, efficient, stable)\n\n**Suspension Types:**\n- Hardtail (front suspension only): smooth trails, cross-country\n- Full-suspension (front and rear): technical trails, rough terrain\n\n**Frame Material:**\n- Aluminum (lightweight, affordable)\n- Carbon fiber (lightweight, high-performance)\n- Steel (durable, comfortable)\n\n**Brakes:** Disc brakes essential; choose mechanical or hydraulic.\n\n**Tires:**\n- Tread: aggressive for technical, smoother for cross-country\n- Width: 2.3-2.5" for traction, 1.9-2.2" for speed\n\n**Components:** Consider fork, rear shock, wheels quality.\n\n**Accessories:** Dropper post, pedals, saddle.\n\n**Dropper Post Benefits:**\n- Increased control and confidence on descents\n- Improved clearance on rough terrain\n- Enhanced descending capabilities\n- Efficient climbing (raise for pedaling)\n- Convenient on-the-fly adjustment\n\n**For beginners:** Consider hardtail or entry-level full-suspension with forgiving geometry (relaxed head angle, lower standover, shorter top tube).\n\n**Important:** Always test ride before buying.
