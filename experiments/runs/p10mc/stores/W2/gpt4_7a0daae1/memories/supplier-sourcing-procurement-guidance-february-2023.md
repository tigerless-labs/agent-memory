## Supplier Sourcing & Procurement Guidance (February 26, 2023)

**User Context:**
- Actively finding suppliers for their products
- Been in touch with multiple suppliers over the past week
- Looking to formalize supplier relationships through contracts and quality processes

**Topics Covered:**

### 1. Supplier Contract Template
Received comprehensive template including sections for:
- Introduction (parties and purpose)
- Scope of work (product/service description, specs, quality standards)
- Pricing and payment terms
- Delivery and shipping (FOB, CIF, DDP terms)
- Quality and warranty
- Intellectual property protection
- Term and termination clauses
- Liability and indemnification
- Dispute resolution
- Confidentiality and non-disclosure
- Governing law and jurisdiction
- Key tips: clearly define scope, establish quality standards, specify payment schedule, address IP concerns, define term/termination, establish dispute resolution

### 2. Price Negotiation Strategies
Key tactics provided:
- Research market rates and competitor pricing
- Know requirements and non-negotiables
- Build supplier relationships
- Be transparent about budget constraints
- Politely ask for better prices with phrases like "Is this the best price you can offer?"
- Look for value-added services (free shipping, priority delivery, technical support)
- Be willing to walk away
- Use competition as leverage by sharing competitor quotes
- Consider alternative pricing models: tiered pricing, cost-plus pricing, value-based pricing
- Verify and confirm final terms in writing

### 3. Supplier Evaluation Framework
Key factors to assess:
- Product/service quality and certifications
- Reliability and consistency (on-time delivery, track record)
- Pricing competitiveness and transparency
- Customer service and support quality
- Financial stability and industry reputation
- Supply chain visibility and risk management
- Relevant certifications (ISO, CE, FDA)
- Innovation and continuous improvement

Red flags to watch:
- Poor communication or unresponsiveness
- Lack of transparency in pricing/quality
- Unwillingness to provide references or case studies
- History of late deliveries, defects, quality issues
- Inadequate insurance or liability protection
- Unprofessional behavior or unqualified staff
- Unclear or unfair contract terms
- Unwillingness to adapt to changing needs

### 4. Supplier Onboarding Process
Steps outlined:
1. Create comprehensive onboarding checklist tailored to needs
2. Finalize and sign contract/agreement
3. Gather supplier profile information (company details, product specs, certifications, insurance)
4. Designate primary point of contact and establish communication channels
5. Schedule onboarding meeting/call (introduce team, review expectations, discuss protocols)
6. Provide training on business processes and quality standards
7. Conduct testing and quality assurance checks
8. Establish payment terms and invoicing procedures
9. Set up KPIs to measure supplier performance

Recommended tools:
- ContractWorks, Concord (contract management)
- SAP Ariba, Coupa (supplier management systems)
- Asana, Trello (project management/collaboration)
- Qualityze, ETQ (quality management)
- Excel/Google Sheets for checklists and performance templates

### 5. Quality Control Process Implementation
Steps for establishing QC:
1. Define clear quality standards and specifications
2. Identify critical control points in supplier's process
3. Develop quality control plan with procedures and inspection frequency
4. Implement regular inspections and testing (use statistical process control/Six Sigma)
5. Establish corrective action process for defects (root cause analysis)
6. Monitor and evaluate supplier performance using KPIs (defect rates, on-time delivery, satisfaction)

Checks and balances:
- Regular audits and inspections
- Third-party audits
- Certification compliance (ISO 9001, etc.)
- Customer feedback and complaint monitoring

Relevant standards:
- ISO 9001:2015 (Quality Management Systems)
- AS9100 (Aerospace Quality Management)
- Six Sigma (statistical process control)
- Lean Manufacturing (process optimization)

**User Intent:**
Planning to use this guidance to finalize supplier contracts, negotiate prices with existing supplier contacts, evaluate options, establish formal onboarding procedures, and implement quality control measures.
