## Employer Best Practices for Disability-Inclusive Interviews

### Key Recommendations

1. **Communicate beforehand** — Ask applicants if they require accommodations (wheelchair accessibility, sign language interpretation, extra time). Follow up to ensure needs are met.

2. **Be aware of biases** — Ensure selection is based on job-relevant skills, knowledge, and experience rather than disability.

3. **Provide reasonable accommodations** — Offer assistive technology, interpreters, or other requested accommodations.

4. **Create welcoming environment** — Provide clear directions, offer water/snacks, provide breaks, ensure physical comfort.

5. **Train interviewers** — Educate staff about disability etiquette and effective communication with people with disabilities.

6. **Gather feedback** — Ask candidates for feedback on their interview experience and incorporate suggestions for improvement.

### Why Gaps Exist

- **Lack of awareness** — Many employers don't understand unique needs of people with disabilities or how to accommodate them correctly
- **Outdated biases** — Some hold outdated or negative beliefs about the abilities of people with disabilities
- **Lack of prioritization** — Some employers don't see value in hiring people with disabilities and don't prioritize inclusion
- **Note:** These gaps may reflect lack of awareness or effort rather than purely lack of care; deep biases take time to change

### Business Case for Inclusion

Creating inclusive workplaces benefits all employees and the business long-term. Promoting diversity, equity, and inclusion creates a more positive workplace environment overall.
