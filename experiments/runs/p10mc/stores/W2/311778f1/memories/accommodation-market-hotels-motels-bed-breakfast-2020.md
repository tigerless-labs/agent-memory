## Accommodation Market Overview (2020)

**Hotels vs Motels**
- Hotels: larger facilities, multiple rooms, amenities (spa, fine dining, views as destination)
- Motels: smaller rooms, direct parking lot access from units, laundry facilities
- Motels closer to roadways for highway convenience
- Guest segments: motels used for road trip stopovers; hotels compete for all traveler segments

**Bed & Breakfasts (B&Bs)**
- Much smaller, owner-operator model
- Typically couple of rooms in owner's own home
- Differentiator: cozy, home-like, personalized, quieter atmosphere vs hotels/motels
- Often located in rural areas (cost-prohibitive for large hotel investment)
- Appeal: historical properties, ambiance, slower pace, community connection
- Investment: owner-operated homes/properties with character and allure

**Industry Cost Structure (% of Revenue)**

| Cost Item | Hotels/Motels | Bed & Breakfasts |
|-----------|---------------|-----------------|
| Wages | 26% | 28% |
| Purchases | 18% | 12% |
| Depreciation | 9% | 5% |
| Marketing | 2% | 2% |
| Rent & Utilities | 7% | 10% |
| Other | 27% | 40% |
| **Total** | **78%** | **78%** |

**Market Segments (US, 2020)**
- Recreation: 70%
- Business: 18%
- Other (meetings, etc): 12%

**Regulatory Environment**
- All establishments subject to city, state, federal regulations
- Coverage: physical property, food safety, disability access, alcohol distribution
- Operating requirements: licenses, fees
- Taxation: properties and revenues subject to state/federal tax

**Expansion Constraints**
- Physical construction prevents rapid expansion
- International growth requires purchasing existing locations or M&A
- Example: Marriott International's 2016 acquisition of Starwood Hotels & Resorts
