## Visual Adaptations
- **Compound eyes with enlarged lenses** — collect more light, increase sensitivity to low levels
- **Tapetum lucidum** — light-reflecting structure in the retina that bounces light back through photoreceptors, enhancing sensitivity in very low-light conditions

## Chemical Navigation
- **Pheromones** — used to locate potential mates
- **Plant chemical detection** — find food sources by detecting volatile compounds released by plants

## Tactile Navigation
- **Sensilla** — specialized sensory structures on antennae and legs that detect environmental changes and help navigate around obstacles

## Attraction to Artificial Light ("Moth to a Flame")
Two leading theories:
1. **Celestial Navigation Confusion** — moths navigate by the moon and stars; artificial lights disorient them by mimicking celestial cues, causing them to fly toward the light source
2. **Positive Phototaxis** — moths are innately drawn to brighter areas, which evolutionarily indicated feeding or mating opportunities

Note: While this behavior seems maladaptive, most moth populations aren't significantly impacted by artificial lights overall, though some species are severely affected.
