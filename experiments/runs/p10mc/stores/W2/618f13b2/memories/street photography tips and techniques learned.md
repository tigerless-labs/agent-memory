## Street Photography Tips and Techniques

### Approaching Strangers

**Respectful approach:**
- Be kind, respectful, and genuine
- Make eye contact and smile
- Be clear and concise ("Excuse me, I'm a photographer and I love your [outfit/accessory/pose]. May I take your photo?")
- Be prepared for rejection — don't take it personally
- Read body language and respect boundaries
- Show your work on phone/camera if asked
- Keep interactions brief
- Be aware of surroundings and context

### Building Rapport

- Show genuine interest in the person
- Make eye contact and use open body language
- Introduce yourself and explain your project
- Be prepared to answer questions
- Be sensitive to cultural and personal differences
- Follow up and show appreciation
- Start with small talk during commutes
- Look for common ground

### Capturing Candid Moments

**Composition techniques:**
- Observe and anticipate behavior
- Pay attention to light (natural, artificial, shadows)
- Use environment to add context and interest
- Play with layers of depth (foreground, middle ground, background)
- Experiment with negative space
- Focus on gestures and expressions
- Shoot from the hip
- Use zone focusing (pre-focus and wait)
- Shoot in burst mode
- Wait for the "decisive moment" (Henri Cartier-Bresson concept)
- Focus on the eyes
- Experiment with unusual angles

### Low-Profile Shooting

- Blend in with neutral, casual clothing
- Know your equipment settings intimately
- Shoot from hip or chest level
- Use small, compact lenses (like 50mm)
- Avoid drawing attention
- Stay alert and flexible

### Lighting and Timing

- Soft, natural light brings out facial expression subtleties
- Avoid harsh mid-day sun
- Overcast conditions work well
- Take advantage of peak commute hours
- Use interesting vantage points (staircases, escalators, bridges)

### 50mm f/1.8 Lens Specific Tips

- Take advantage of shallow depth of field to isolate subjects
- Compact and discreet — helps blend in
- Get close but respect personal space
- Fast aperture helps in lower light and for discreet shooting

### General Best Practices

- Practice consistently
- Respect people's privacy and personal space
- Capture the mundane, not just extraordinary moments
- Be aware of surroundings and keep belongings secure
- Know local laws regarding photography in public spaces
