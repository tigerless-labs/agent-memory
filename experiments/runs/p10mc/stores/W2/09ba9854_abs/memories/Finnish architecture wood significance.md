## Significance of Wood in Traditional Finnish Architecture

Wood is central to Finnish architecture for multiple reasons:

1. Availability — Finland's vast forests provide abundant timber
2. Insulation — Natural properties help buildings withstand harsh winters
3. Sustainability — Renewable resource with low carbon footprint
4. Versatility — Easily shaped for diverse architectural forms
5. Cultural Symbol — Wood represents Finnish identity and heritage

## Regional Architectural Traditions

- Savonia (eastern Finland): wooden church villages from 17th-18th centuries, blend of eastern and western styles
- Karelia (eastern Finland): long history of wooden farmsteads and traditional houses
- Northern Finland: Sami people originally built lavvus (teepee-like structures), later developed permanent wooden housing
- Helsinki, Turku, Rauma: historic neighborhoods with preserved traditional styles

## Similar Traditions Across Nordic and Baltic Regions

- Sweden and Norway: strong wooden architecture with sloping roofs, gable ends, decorative details
- Iceland: turf houses built with turf and rock for insulation
- Estonia and Latvia: traditional wooden buildings with intricate wood carvings and ornate gable decorations
