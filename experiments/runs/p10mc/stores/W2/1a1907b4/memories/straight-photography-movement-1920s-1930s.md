Photography style/movement from early 20th century, particularly 1920s-1930s. Characterized by straightforward, objective approach without manipulation or artificial effects. Sought to capture world truthfully and honestly without embellishments or distortions.

Term coined by Alfred Stieglitz, key figure in development. Stieglitz believed photography should be treated as art form in its own right while maintaining honesty and truthfulness. Influenced by Pictorialism (artistic photography) and documentary tradition (social/political focus), but rejected artificial effects.

Notable photographers in movement: Edward Weston, Paul Strand, Ansel Adams. Often featured simple subjects (landscapes, still lifes, portraits) approached with clarity, precision, attention to detail. Committed to objectivity and power of photographic image to capture essence of the world.

Legacy: influence seen in street, landscape, portrait photography today. Celebrated for commitment to objectivity and truthfulness.
