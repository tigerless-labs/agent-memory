McCain's healthcare reform philosophy was built on several core principles:

**Market-based approach**: He believed in expanding choice and competition in healthcare to drive down costs and increase access. He supported greater consumer control over insurance options and decision-making.

**Preventative care and chronic disease**: He endorsed initiatives to improve coordination of care for patients with chronic conditions, which account for a significant portion of healthcare spending.

**Medicaid reform**: Generally supportive of Medicaid but believed it needed significant reforms to address cost and quality issues. He was critical of reimbursement rates and wanted greater state flexibility in administering the program.

**Key positions**:
- Opposed the ACA individual mandate (requirement for all Americans to have health insurance)
- Opposed single-payer systems like Medicare for All
- Somewhat receptive to a public option as a tool for increasing competition in insurance markets
- In 2017, cast the deciding Senate vote to block Republican repeal of the ACA, stating the bill did not address rising healthcare costs or lack of access

McCain died in 2018.
