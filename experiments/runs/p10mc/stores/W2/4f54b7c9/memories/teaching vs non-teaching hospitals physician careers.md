## Teaching Hospitals
- Affiliated with medical schools; prioritize education and training of residents/medical students
- More structured schedules (rounds, lectures, conferences)
- Focus on both patient care AND teaching/research
- Higher workloads, pressure to balance clinical + teaching + research
- May require longer hours including nights/weekends
- Less schedule flexibility
- More opportunities for professional development and mentorship
- Access to cutting-edge treatments, technology, specialized care
- Often located in urban areas (higher cost of living)
- May have lower salaries compared to non-teaching hospitals
- ~20% of all US hospitals; higher percentage in urban areas
- Serve as centers for medical innovation and research

## Non-Teaching Hospitals
- Primary focus on patient care
- More flexible schedules
- Smaller, more specialized teams
- May have salary advantages
- Fewer opportunities for education/mentorship
- Fewer resources, less access to cutting-edge technology
- Physicians may feel isolated from academic community
- Fewer opportunities for career advancement
