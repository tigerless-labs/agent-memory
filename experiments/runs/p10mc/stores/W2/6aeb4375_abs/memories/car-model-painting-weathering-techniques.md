## Car Model Painting and Weathering Techniques

**Painting Process:**
1. Choose acrylic or enamel model paint (Tamiya, Vallejo, Testors brands)
2. Prime entire model with gray or white primer for better adhesion
3. Apply base coat with high-gloss paint (e.g., Tamiya TS-14 Candy Apple Red for muscle cars)
4. Use masking tape or film for sharp lines on stripes and emblems
5. Apply clear coat for protection and glossy finish

**Weathering Effects:**
1. Dust and dirt - apply wash or pigment mix to wheels, tires, lower panels
2. Tire wear - use graphite pencil or dark gray marker on sidewalls and tread
3. Chips and scratches - small brush with lighter base color shade
4. Rust and corrosion - rust-colored pigments on undercarriage, wheel wells, hinges
5. Chrome and glass - silver marker for chrome, clear acrylic for glass

**Best Practices:**
- Study reference images of real vehicles
- Practice on test pieces before applying to actual model
- Build weathering effects in layers, starting subtle
- Use magnifying glass or lamp for precision on small details
- Remember less is more with weathering (avoid over-weathering)
- Let each layer dry before applying next

**Specific to Muscle Cars:**
- Bold vibrant colors (e.g., Candy Apple Red for 1969 Camaro)
- Consider period-accurate weathering
- Hood stripes and emblems require careful masking
