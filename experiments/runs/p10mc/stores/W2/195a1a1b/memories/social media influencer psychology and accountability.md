## Influencer Success Factors
- **Authenticity**: perceived as genuine, sharing weaknesses and strengths
- **Relatability**: content mirrors followers' experiences and values, builds trust
- **Social influence**: followers view them as role models, influencers shift opinions and purchasing behavior
- **User-generated content**: followers create content alongside influencers, builds community
- **Consistency**: regular posts and follower interaction creates stability and reliability

## Downsides & Risks
- **Misinformation**: influencers can spread false information
- **Disordered eating**: unrealistic body types and rigid diet/exercise routines contribute to eating disorders
- **Materialism**: promotion of excessive consumerism and material possession focus
- **Mental health**: burnout for influencers and followers; pressure to keep up
- **Digital addiction**: young people especially vulnerable to heavy device dependence

## Accountability & Regulation Issues
- FTC has created guidelines for sponsored content transparency, but enforcement is weak
- Many influencers ignore guidelines and don't disclose product risks/side effects
- Social media platforms implement minimal monitoring despite requests for responsibility
- Companies often prioritize profits over user wellbeing

## What Individuals Can Do
1. **Educate yourself** on how social media works, data collection, platform policies
2. **Speak up** to friends, family, lawmakers about concerns
3. **Use purchasing power** — switch to platforms aligned with your values
4. **Support responsible creators** who prioritize mental health and wellbeing
5. **Join advocacy groups** working on social media and tech regulation

## Key Insight
Individual actions multiply when people join together; grassroots civil action can force corporations and governments to align with public interest — change is possible, not inevitable powerlessness.
