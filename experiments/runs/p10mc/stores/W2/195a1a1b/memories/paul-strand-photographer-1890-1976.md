## Paul Strand (1890-1976)

American photographer and pioneer of modernist photography in early 20th century. Key figure in the straight photography movement and documentary photography development.

**Life:**
- Born in New York City
- Largely self-taught
- Strongly influenced by Alfred Stieglitz and other modernist photographers
- Joined Stieglitz's Photo-Secession group in 1915
- Began exhibiting work in their gallery

**Career:**
- 1920s: Shifted focus to documentary photography
- Traveled extensively photographing:
  - Everyday life
  - Social issues
  - Rural communities
  - Urban landscapes
  - Industrial sites
- Photographs characterized by simplicity, directness, and social purpose
- Starting 1932: Famous series of photographs of Mexico's people and landscapes—considered some of the most important examples of documentary photography from the period

**Philosophy:**
- Deeply committed to using photography as a powerful tool for social change
- Believed he could raise awareness of social injustices and inspire action through his photographs

**Legacy:**
- Established as one of the leading photographers of his generation
- Work celebrated for innovative style, social relevance, and enduring influence on modern photography development
