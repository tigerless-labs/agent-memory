## Reasons for Opposition to Civil Rights Act of 1964

### Main Arguments Made by Opponents

1. **States' Rights**: Many opponents claimed that the Civil Rights Act violated states' rights by giving the federal government too much power over state and local affairs; preferred to exercise their own discretion rather than have federal government mandate change

2. **Economic Impact**: Some believed the Act would have negative economic impact by forcing businesses to hire minorities and accommodate their needs, resulting in higher costs

3. **Individual Liberties**: Some claimed the Act violated individual liberties by forcing private businesses to serve customers they didn't want to serve

4. **Racism and Prejudice**: Some people simply believed in and practiced racism and prejudice, and did not want to see it end

### Opposition Groups and Figures

- **Southern Democrats ("segregationists")** — primary opposition
- **Senator Strom Thurmond** (South Carolina) — led filibuster in Senate to block legislation
- **Senator James Eastland** (Mississippi)
- **Senator Albert Gore Sr.** (Tennessee)
- **White supremacist groups** — including the Ku Klux Klan

### Historical Note

Despite this significant opposition, the Civil Rights Act of 1964 ultimately passed with bipartisan support. President Johnson garnered support from Northern Democrats and Republicans to overcome the Southern filibuster.

**Modern perspective:** Most Americans today agree that the Civil Rights Act of 1964 was a vital step in ensuring equal rights and protections for all individuals, regardless of race, sex, religion, or national origin. The arguments against it are now widely recognized as based on flawed logic, misinformation, and fear rather than rational reasoning.
