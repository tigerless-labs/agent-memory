## Civil Rights Act of 1964

**Date signed:** July 2, 1964 by President Lyndon B. Johnson

**Scope of prohibitions:** Discrimination based on race, color, religion, sex, or national origin

**Coverage areas:**
- Public facilities (segregation and discrimination outlawed)
- Employment
- Education

**Key provisions:**
- Established the Equal Employment Opportunity Commission (EEOC) to investigate and enforce anti-discrimination laws in the workplace
- Gave federal government power to withhold funding from states or local governments that continued to discriminate against minority groups

**Historical significance:** Landmark legislation that ushered in a new era of civil rights and social justice; paved the way for subsequent legislation including the Voting Rights Act of 1965 and the Fair Housing Act of 1968

**Opposition:** Southern Democrats (segregationists), including Senator Strom Thurmond (led filibuster), James Eastland of Mississippi, Albert Gore Sr. of Tennessee; white supremacist groups like the KKK; opponents argued it violated states' rights and individual freedom

**Passage:** Despite significant opposition, passed with bipartisan support — Johnson garnered support from Northern Democrats and Republicans to overcome Southern filibuster
