## Straight Photography

Photographic style that emerged in the early 20th century (1920s-1930s), characterized by straightforward and objective approach to photography without manipulation or artistic effects.

**Key Characteristics:**
- Truthful, honest, objective representations of the world
- Rejected artificial effects or manipulation
- Sought to capture the world as it really was, without embellishments or distortions
- Often featured simple, everyday subjects: landscapes, still lifes, portraits
- Approached with clarity, precision, and attention to detail

**Influences:**
- Influenced by Pictorialism (emphasized artistic qualities of photography)
- Influenced by documentary tradition (focused on social and political issues)
- But rejected the artificial effects of these movements

**Key Figures:**
- **Alfred Stieglitz** — coined the term "straight photography," believed photography should be treated as an art form in its own right
- **Edward Weston** — pioneer of the style
- **Paul Strand** — pioneer of the style
- **Ansel Adams** — pioneer of the style

**Legacy:**
- Influence seen in many modern genres: street photography, landscape, portrait photography
- Celebrated for commitment to objectivity, truthfulness, and power of photographic image
