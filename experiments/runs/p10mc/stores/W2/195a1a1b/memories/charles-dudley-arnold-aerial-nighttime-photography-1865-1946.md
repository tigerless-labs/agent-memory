## Charles Dudley Arnold (1865-1946)

American photographer known for pioneering work in aerial and nighttime photography. One of the first photographers to experiment with capturing images from balloons and airplanes.

**Life:**
- Born in New York City
- Studied photography at Art Students League of New York
- Born 1865
- Died 1946

**Career Start:**
- Began career as portrait photographer
- Became interested in capturing images from unusual perspectives

**Aerial Photography:**
- 1890s: Began experimenting with aerial photography using camera mounted on balloon
- Early 1900s: Began using airplanes as platform for photography, one of first to do so
- Worked with pioneering aviators including **Orville Wright**
- Used variety of cameras and techniques to capture images from air
- Photographs characterized by innovative composition and ability to capture landscape patterns and textures from unique perspective

**Nighttime Photography:**
- Pioneering work capturing photographs at night (unusual for the time period)
- Experimented with various techniques for low-light photography
- Used artificial light sources: electric lights, gas lamps, torches
- Developed special equipment:
  - Flash powder
  - Magnesium wire
  - Various tools to produce bursts of bright light for complete darkness photography

**Nighttime Subjects:**
- Often photographed urban scenes: city streets and buildings
- Dramatic, moody, atmospheric effects
- Sharp contrasts between light and dark
- Captured mystery and drama of night

**Legacy:**
- Helped establish aerial photography as new and important genre
- Pioneering effort in nighttime photography
- Work influenced other photographers exploring night photography possibilities
- Photographs recognized as important historical documents and works of art
- Continue to inspire and influence photographers worldwide
