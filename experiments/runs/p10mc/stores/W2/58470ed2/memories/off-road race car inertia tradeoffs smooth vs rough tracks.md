## Design Principles

**Lower inertias** benefit smooth off-road tracks:
- Increase agility and maneuverability
- Reduce response time in tight/twisty sections
- Better for quick directional changes

**Higher inertias** benefit rough off-road tracks:
- Improve stability and control
- Resist rollover and maintain traction
- Better for maintaining line at speed over rough terrain

## Finding the Right Compromise

The optimal balance depends on:
- Specific vehicle design and components
- Track layout and surface conditions
- Driver preferences and skill

**Definitions:**
- **Roll inertia**: resistance to rotation around longitudinal axis (important for rollover resistance)
- **Polar inertia**: resistance to rotation around vertical axis (important for high-speed cornering stability)
- **CG height**: center of gravity position; lower CG improves stability and reduces rollover susceptibility

## Testing Methodology

1. Gather track data: layout, surface conditions, performance requirements
2. Create mathematical model to predict vehicle behavior
3. Experimentally adjust inertias (via suspension, body shape, component placement)
4. Conduct on-track testing with actual vehicle
5. Iterate design and testing until optimal compromise achieved

The best compromise requires actual on-track validation under real conditions, as behavior varies significantly between smooth and rough surfaces.
