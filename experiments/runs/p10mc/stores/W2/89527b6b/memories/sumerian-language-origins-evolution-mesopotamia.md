## Origins
Sumerian is believed to be one of the earliest written languages in human history, dating back to 4000 BC. It originated in the Sumerian civilization in Mesopotamia (modern-day Iraq), considered the world's first civilization.

## Writing System
Initially written on clay tablets using cuneiform script—a system of wedge-shaped symbols impressed onto clay surfaces. Fired clay tablets preserved over centuries.

## Uses
- **Administrative**: recording trade transactions, livestock/crop accounts, legal disputes
- **Religious**: hymns, prayers, rituals

## Evolution and Decline
As Mesopotamian society evolved, the language influenced neighboring languages including Akkadian, Babylonian, and Assyrian. With the rise of the Babylonian and Assyrian empires, Sumerian lost dominance and was replaced by Akkadian and Aramaic. However, it remained important for scholarship and religion.

## Surviving Works
Many Sumerian writings survive on clay tablets, primarily:
- Religious texts: hymns, poems, prayers, incantations
- Administrative/legal documents: contracts, deeds, receipts
- **Epic of Gilgamesh**: one of the earliest surviving works of literature in the world

## Current Status
Sumerian is considered extinct. Only studied by scholars and researchers today, but remains crucial to understanding Mesopotamian history and culture.
