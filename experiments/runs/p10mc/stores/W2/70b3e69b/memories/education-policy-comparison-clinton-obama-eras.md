## Clinton Era Educational Policies

- **Goals 2000** — aimed to improve academic standards and student achievement by setting goals for schools and districts; generally well-received
- **School-to-Work Opportunities Act** — encouraged school-business partnerships to provide vocational education and training
- **No Child Left Behind (NCLB)** — signed by Bush but initially proposed by Clinton in 1994; mandated annual testing and accountability; raised awareness of achievement gaps with modest positive impacts on student achievement, but criticized for over-focus on standardized testing

## Obama Era Educational Policies

- **Race to the Top** — incentivized states to adopt education reform, implement high academic standards, tie teacher evaluations to student performance; showed some positive impacts
- **Common Core State Standards** — aimed to establish consistent academic standards across states; faced backlash from some educators and parents
- **Every Student Succeeds Act (ESSA)** — signed 2015, largely replaced NCLB, gave states more control over education policy and assessment

## Key Lessons from Both Eras

1. Clear performance goals and standardized measures useful for identifying improvements and accountability
2. Teacher preparation, professional development, and financial incentives matter
3. School-business partnerships provide real-world experience and employment opportunities
4. Must address educational disparities with targeted support for disadvantaged students
5. Successful policy requires multifaceted approach accommodating diverse needs

## Biden Administration (mentioned 2023)

- Pledged $775 billion investment over 10 years
- Focus on Title I schools (low-income communities)
- Teacher preparation and support
- Expand dual-enrollment and community college access
- Student loan debt forgiveness and free community college
- Emphasis on educational research, innovation, new assessment approaches
- Address systemic inequalities, diversify teaching workforce
