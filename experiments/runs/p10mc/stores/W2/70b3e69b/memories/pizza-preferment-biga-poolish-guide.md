## Pizza Preferments: Biga, Poolish, and Sponge

**What is a preferment:**
A small amount of dough that ferments before being added to main dough. Develops natural yeast and enzymes, breaks down starches, produces flavor compounds and aroma.

**Preferment types:**

### Biga (Italian)
- Made with flour, water, and sometimes yeast
- Fermentation time: 12-24 hours
- Develops more complex flavor and aroma

**24-hour biga:**
- Pros: more complex flavor, deeper/more mature taste, better crust texture, breaks down more starches
- Cons: time-consuming, higher risk of over-fermentation
- Use when: want maximum complexity and crust improvement

**12-hour biga:**
- Pros: faster (prepare morning, use evening), easier to manage, reduced over-fermentation risk, still beneficial
- Cons: less complex flavor, less crust texture improvement than 24-hour
- Use when: shorter on time or want convenience

### Poolish (French)
- Higher water content than biga
- Fermentation time: 1-4 hours
- More liquid consistency

### Sponge
- Higher yeast content
- Fermentation time: 1-2 hours
- More rapid fermentation

**Benefits of using preferments:**
1. **More complex flavor**: develops fruit, nuts, spice notes
2. **Better crust texture**: starches break down, leads to crisper crust
3. **Improved dough strength**: develops gluten network, stronger/more resilient dough

**How to incorporate into pizza dough:**
1. Create preferment: mix flour, water, yeast (optional depending on type), cover, ferment at room temperature (75-78 degrees F)
2. Add fermented preferment to rest of dough ingredients, mix until incorporated
3. Adjust recipe: may need to adjust yeast, salt, and water amounts based on preferment type

**Example biga recipe:**
- 100g bread flour
- 100g water
- 0.1g active dry yeast (optional)
- Ferment at room temperature for 24 hours

**Example pizza dough with biga:**
- 1000g bread flour
- 650g water
- 20g salt
- 10g active dry yeast
- 100g biga (after fermentation)
- Mix ingredients, add fermented biga, mix until incorporated, proceed as usual

**Tips:**
- Mix just until shaggy mass (don't overmix)
- Let dough rest and develop over time
- Monitor fermentation progress
- Adjust fermentation time based on temperature and yeast activity
