## Kiowa Language Revitalization

### Historical Barriers
- U.S. government forced Indigenous peoples onto reservations (late 1800s)
- Boarding schools prohibited speaking native languages
- Children removed from families and communities, causing intergenerational trauma
- English dominance led younger generations to abandon Kiowa for assimilation

### Revitalization Efforts
**Kiowa Tribe programs:**
- Language immersion programs in schools
- Online learning resources
- Language preservation workshops
- Dictionaries and written materials by elders and native speakers
- Kiowa Tribe Language Immersion Program in Anadarko, Oklahoma — positive results in language acquisition and cultural identity

### Resources for Learning & Support
1. **Kiowa Language Program** — Kiowa Tribe of Oklahoma; provides classes, materials, resources
2. **Kiowa Conversations podcast** — Fluent speakers having conversations in Kiowa (Kiowa Tribe Language Department)
3. **Dictionary of the Kiowa Language** by Jack Gladstone — 7,000+ entries; available on Amazon
4. **Native American Language Preservation and Maintenance website** — Comprehensive information on Indigenous language revitalization efforts across North America

### Comparable Efforts in Other Native American Languages
- **Navajo** — Immersion schools, summer camps, online resources; increased speakers
- **Cherokee** — 25+ years of preservation through immersion schools, language camps, resources
- **Ojibwe** — Language immersion schools, community classes, documentation of native speakers (Minnesota, Wisconsin)
- **Hawaiian** — Nearly extinct mid-20th century; now thousands of fluent speakers; revitalization through immersion schools and learning resources

### Supporting Language Revitalization
Non-community members can support by:
- Learning and using Indigenous languages
- Providing financial resources
- Advocating for language revitalization policies
- Offering assistance in documentation/translation
- Promoting cultural awareness and respect
