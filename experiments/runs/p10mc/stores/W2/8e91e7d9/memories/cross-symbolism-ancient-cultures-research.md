## Cross Symbolism Across Ancient Cultures

### Egyptian (Ankh)
- Symbol of life, immortality, and balance
- Four elements of nature with corresponding deities
- Horizontal arm = earthly realm; vertical arm = spiritual realm
- Intersection = joining of realms, balance between them
- Associated with regeneration and eternal life
- Highly prized symbol in art and religious iconography

### Phoenician
- Cross linked to goddess Astarte (fertility, sexuality, love)
- Represented four directions of the world
- Symbolized balance and harmony of natural world
- Four elements: earth, air, fire, water
- Used in temple decoration, jewelry, pottery, textiles

### Greek (Tau)
- Associated with god Tammuz/Dionysus (wine and fertility)
- Represented resurrection and regeneration
- Believed to have sacred and protective power
- Used in architecture and art

### Roman
- Initially symbol of punishment and crucifixion of criminals

### Christian
- Central icon of Christianity
- Represents sacrifice and resurrection of Jesus Christ
- Vertical bar = divine realm; horizontal bar = earthly realm
- Symbolizes unity of God and humanity
- Symbol of hope, forgiveness, eternal life
- Forms: Latin cross, Greek cross, Celtic cross
- Used in baptism, communion, and religious ceremonies

### Hindu & Buddhist
- Hindu: intersection of physical and spiritual realms; associated with Vishnu
- Buddhist: crossed Buddha arms = four immeasurables (love, compassion, joy, equanimity)

### Jewish (Kabbalah)
- Represents Tree of Life in Kabbalah tradition
