Thrombus Morphology Features

Color
- Fresh thrombi: Red or dark red, high proportion of red blood cells
- Older thrombi: Pale, after fibrinolysis or organization

Consistency
- Firm: Composed of fibrin and platelets, less likely to embolize
- Friable: Composed of red blood cells and fibrin, more likely to break off and travel

Shape
- Cylindrical, conical, or branching patterns
- Depends on location and blood flow dynamics

Attachment
- Adherent: Attached to vessel wall, more likely to cause obstruction and tissue damage
- Non-adherent: Floating in vessel lumen, higher risk of embolism

Organization (Over Time)
Thrombus undergoes infiltration by:
- Fibroblasts
- Blood vessels (neovascularization)
- Eventually transforms into fibrous scar
- Can partially or completely occlude vessel

Recanalization
- Partial or complete dissolution of thrombus
- Formation of channel/lumen within thrombus
- Restores some degree of blood flow in affected vessel

Clinical Significance

Morphology helps:
- Diagnose underlying cause
- Predict embolism or recurrence risk
- Guide treatment choice
