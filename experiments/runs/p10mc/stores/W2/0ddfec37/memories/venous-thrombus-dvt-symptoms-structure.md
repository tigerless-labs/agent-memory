Venous Thrombus: Structure, Symptoms, and Location

Definition
Venous thrombus is a blood clot forming in the venous system, typically in deep veins of legs or pelvis. Consists of mixture: platelets, fibrin, red blood cells, leukocytes. Size and shape vary.

Structure by Age Phase

ACUTE PHASE
- Primarily composed of red blood cells and fibrin
- Relatively soft and friable (breaks apart easily)
- High risk of embolization

ORGANIZED PHASE (Over time)
- Network of capillaries develops (neovascularization)
- Becomes more fibrotic
- Can undergo recanalization

Common Location
- Deep veins of the legs (most common)
- Pelvic veins
- Risk of migration to lungs causing pulmonary embolism

Symptoms of Deep Vein Thrombosis (DVT)

Symptomatic presentation may include:
- Swelling in affected limb
- Pain
- Warmth over clot site
- Redness (erythema)

Important: Many DVT cases are asymptomatic. First sign may be pulmonary embolism.

Pulmonary Embolism (PE) Complications
When clot breaks off and travels to lungs:
- Shortness of breath
- Chest pain
- Severe cases: death
- PE is life-threatening emergency

Incidence
- Reported at up to 1 per 1,000 person-years in the population
