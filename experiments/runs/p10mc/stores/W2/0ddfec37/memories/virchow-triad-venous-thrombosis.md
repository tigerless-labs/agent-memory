Virchow's Triad: Three Factors in Venous Thrombus Formation

Virchow's triad explains venous thrombi formation through three pathophysiologic mechanisms:

FACTOR 1: Stasis of Blood Flow
When blood flow is slow or stagnant, clot formation risk increases.
Causes: immobility, varicose veins, post-surgical conditions, atrial fibrillation

FACTOR 2: Endothelial Injury
Damage to blood vessel lining triggers clot formation.
Causes: trauma, infection, toxin exposure, atherosclerosis

FACTOR 3: Hypercoagulability
Increased tendency of blood to clot.

Medical conditions causing hypercoagulability:
- Cancer
- Pregnancy
- Autoimmune disorders
- Genetic clotting disorders (Factor V Leiden mutation, antithrombin III deficiency)

Medications causing hypercoagulability:
- Hormonal therapy
- Oral contraceptives

Clinical Application

Venous thrombosis typically results from combination of these factors, not single factor. Understanding which factors are present guides prevention and treatment. Multiple factors significantly increase DVT risk.

Deep Vein Thrombosis Incidence: up to 1 per 1,000 person-years
