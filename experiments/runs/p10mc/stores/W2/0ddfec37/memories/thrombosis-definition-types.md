## Thrombosis Definition

Thrombosis is the formation of a blood clot (thrombus) within a blood vessel, which can partially or completely obstruct blood flow. The process involves interaction between platelets, coagulation factors, and the endothelial lining of blood vessels.

## Formation Mechanism

**Normal clotting process:**
1. Blood vessel injury activates platelets
2. Platelets aggregate to form a platelet plug
3. Coagulation factors activate to form fibrin mesh
4. Fibrin reinforces the plug, creating a stable clot

**Pathological thrombosis:** Occurs when the clotting process becomes inappropriately activated due to disrupted blood flow or vessel wall damage, leading to thrombus growth and obstruction.

## Classification by Location

1. **Arterial thrombosis** — occurs in arteries carrying oxygenated blood; can cause tissue ischemia/infarction
   - Examples: coronary artery (heart attack), cerebral artery (stroke), peripheral artery (limb ischemia)

2. **Venous thrombosis** — occurs in veins carrying deoxygenated blood; more common than arterial
   - Deep vein thrombosis (DVT) in leg veins; risk of pulmonary embolism if clot breaks off

3. **Cardiac thrombosis** — occurs in heart chambers
   - Associated with atrial fibrillation, dilated cardiomyopathy, artificial heart valves
   - Can cause embolism when clot travels

4. **Microvascular thrombosis** — occurs in small blood vessels (capillaries, arterioles)
   - Associated with thrombotic microangiopathy, DIC, sickle cell disease

## Risk Factors

- Advanced age
- Obesity
- Smoking
- Cancer
- Autoimmune disorders
- Genetic clotting disorders

## Treatment

Anticoagulant medications to prevent further clotting; interventions like thrombectomy or angioplasty to remove/dissolve thrombus.
