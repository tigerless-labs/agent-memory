Starling Forces: Capillary Fluid Dynamics and Edema

Definition
Starling forces describe the movement of fluid across the capillary wall, responsible for edema formation. The capillary wall acts as semi-permeable membrane allowing passage of fluids and solutes.

Two Opposing Pressures

HYDROSTATIC PRESSURE
- Exerted by fluid within the capillary
- Tends to PUSH fluid out of capillary into interstitial space
- Increases: increase in blood pressure, venous congestion

ONCOTIC PRESSURE (Osmotic Pressure)
- Exerted by proteins in plasma (especially albumin)
- Tends to PULL fluid back into capillary
- Decreases: protein loss, malnutrition, liver disease

Normal Balance
Under normal conditions, hydrostatic and oncotic pressures are balanced. No net movement of fluid across capillary wall.

Edema Formation: When Balance Is Lost

IMBALANCE CONDITIONS:
- Increased hydrostatic pressure, OR
- Decreased oncotic pressure

Example: Heart Failure
- Heart unable to pump efficiently
- Causes blood backup in veins
- Increases hydrostatic pressure within capillaries
- Fluid leaks into interstitial space
- Results in edema

Example: Liver Disease
- Decreased albumin production (albumin = protein that maintains oncotic pressure)
- Decreased oncotic pressure within capillaries
- Fluid leaks into interstitial space
- Results in edema

Clinical Significance
Understanding Starling forces explains fluid accumulation in various conditions and guides treatment approach (diuretics for hydrostatic causes, albumin/protein therapy for oncotic causes).
