## Interest Areas
- **Mochi:** Traditional Japanese rice cake made from glutinous rice flour, typically filled with red bean paste, sweetened chestnut paste, or ice cream
- **Manju:** Traditional Japanese steamed bun filled with sweet bean paste (azuki beans)
- **Wagashi:** Traditional Japanese sweets/confections served at tea ceremonies and special occasions; emphasizes seasonal ingredients, presentation, and delicate flavors; history dates back to Heian period (794-1185 CE)

## Mochi Recipe Provided
Ingredients:
- 2 cups glutinous rice flour (mochigome)
- 1 cup water
- 1/2 cup granulated sugar
- Filling of choice (red bean paste, sweetened chestnut paste, or ice cream)

Tips:
- Soak rice flour in water for at least 4 hours or overnight
- Use proper ratio of flour to water (2:1)
- Knead dough until smooth and pliable
- Shape and fill as desired

## Manju Recipe Provided
Ingredients:
- 2 cups all-purpose flour
- 1/2 cup granulated sugar
- 1/2 cup warm water
- 1/4 teaspoon salt
- Filling of choice (sweet bean paste made from azuki beans)

Tips:
- Use all-purpose flour or combination with bread flour
- Steam to cook evenly
- Don't overmix dough
- Handle gently to prevent dense texture

## Yokan Recipe Provided
Ingredients:
- 1 cup red bean paste
- 1 cup granulated sugar
- 2 cups water
- 2 tablespoons agar powder
- Food coloring (optional)

Instructions:
1. Prepare red bean paste (canned or homemade)
2. Combine sugar and water, heat over medium heat stirring until dissolved
3. Mix agar powder with water to make smooth paste, add to sugar syrup, stir well
4. Bring to boil, reduce heat to low, simmer 10 minutes until thickened and agar dissolved
5. Add red bean paste, stir to combine
6. Add food coloring if desired
7. Pour into rectangular or individual mold, cool at room temperature
8. Refrigerate until chilled, cut into cubes or slices

Tips:
- Use high-quality agar powder
- Adjust sweetness to taste
- Experiment with flavors (green tea, sesame, yuzu variations)

## Wagashi Information
Types of wagashi:
- **Namagashi:** Fresh sweets made from seasonal ingredients, often served at tea ceremony
- **Higashi:** Dry sweets made from sugar, rice flour, or other ingredients
- **Mochi:** Rice flour-based sweets with fillings

General tips:
- Use seasonal ingredients
- Pay attention to presentation, shape, and color
- Balance flavors (sweetness with salt, umami, acidity)
