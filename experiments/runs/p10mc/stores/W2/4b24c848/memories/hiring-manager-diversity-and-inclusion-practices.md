## Diversity in Hiring & Inclusion

**User role:** Hiring manager seeking to incorporate diverse perspectives in recruitment while navigating biases and systemic barriers.

## Example Initiative

**"Women in National Security"** by Center for a New American Security (CNAS):
- Goal: increase women in national security leadership
- Method: brought together diverse experts from academia, industry, government, military
- Research: analyzed data to identify barriers and opportunities
- Programs: mentorship programs, public awareness events
- Outcomes: raised awareness, led to increased diversity in hiring/promotion practices

**Key insight:** While gender diversity is important, racial and ethnic diversity is equally critical; diverse teams are more innovative and creative.

## 5-Step Process for Inclusive Hiring

1. **Educate yourself** — Learn about bias types and their impact on hiring decisions
2. **Re-evaluate job descriptions** — Use inclusive language, focus on essential skills, remove demographic profiling
3. **Use diverse sourcing methods** — Move beyond referrals; use diversity job boards, community organizations, universities, recruitment events
4. **Implement unconscious bias training** — Provide for all hiring participants; teach practical mitigation strategies
5. **Be transparent about process** — Explain application steps, criteria, interviewer names, reference handling clearly to all candidates

## Key Insight

Meritocracy ideal is flawed because it ignores systemic biases and barriers preventing diverse candidates from building equal qualifications. Inclusive hiring doesn't mean hiring less qualified candidates—it means creating fair processes that level the playing field for equally qualified diverse candidates.
