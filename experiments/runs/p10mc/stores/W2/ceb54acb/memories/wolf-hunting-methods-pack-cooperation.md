Wolf Hunting Methods:
- Pursuit hunting: chase prey for miles using endurance and speed until exhausted
- Ambush hunting: hide in vegetation or rocks, wait for prey to approach
- Pack hunting: use cooperative behavior and superior numbers to surround prey
- Stealth hunting: move low to ground, stalk close before attacking
- Opportunistic hunting: scavenge carrion or hunt smaller prey when large prey is scarce

Solitary vs Pack Hunting:
- Wolves can hunt alone but are much less successful
- Solo hunts typically target smaller and weaker prey only
- Solo hunting is riskier: risk of injury or death from large prey or other predators
- Pack hunting maximizes cooperative strengths and minimizes risks

Pack Cooperation During Hunts:
- Wolves communicate before hunting using vocalizations, physical gestures, scents
- They plan strategy based on prey size/strength, number of wolves, terrain
- Coordinate movements to surround prey and push into vulnerable positions
- Take turns chasing prey to tire it out while others wait in ambush
- Different wolves have different roles: some pursue, others ambush/takedown
- Individual strengths coordinated to maximize success
