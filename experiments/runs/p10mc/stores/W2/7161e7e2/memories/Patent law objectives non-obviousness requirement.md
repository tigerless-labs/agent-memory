## Objectives of Patent Law

1. **Encourage Innovation**: Give inventors exclusive profit rights for a limited time, incentivizing R&D investment
2. **Disseminate Technologies**: Patent disclosure requirements allow public to learn from and build upon inventions
3. **Promote Public Welfare**: Only socially beneficial, new, useful, and non-obvious technologies granted monopoly
4. **Balance Public and Private Interest**: Society can use invention after patent expires; temporary monopoly followed by public access
5. **Encourage Foreign Investment**: Protection of foreign companies' patents in India attracts international investment
6. **Protect SMEs**: Small and medium enterprises receive same patent protection as large corporations, promoting innovation competition
7. **Promote Competition**: Multiple companies can develop competing technologies; prevents single-entity market dominance

## Non-Obviousness Requirement

**Definition**: An invention must not be obvious to a person having ordinary skill in the relevant field of technology.

**Example 1 — Solar Panel**: 
- If a skilled solar technician could easily achieve the same result using known techniques → NOT patentable (obvious)
- If inventor uses completely new material or manufacturing method → IS patentable (non-obvious)

**Example 2 — Water Purification**:
- If a water purification specialist could easily replicate the process using known techniques → NOT patentable (obvious)
- If process uses innovative bacterial approach not derivable by simple experimentation → IS patentable (non-obvious)

**Key Point**: Non-obviousness ensures patents protect genuinely innovative solutions, not trivial modifications of existing technology.
