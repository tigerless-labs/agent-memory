## Bread Flour Usage Techniques

**Key differences from all-purpose flour:**

Protein content:
- Bread flour: 12–14% protein
- All-purpose flour: 10–12% protein

**Effects of higher protein:**

Texture:
- More robust, chewy crumb
- Larger holes in the final product
- More tender yet crispy crust
- Better open, airy crumb (if fermentation/proofing managed well)

Flavor:
- Slightly sweeter flavor
- Slightly nuttier notes
- Complements savory additions (rosemary, herbs)

Gluten development:
- Stronger gluten network
- Better dough elasticity

**Technical adjustments needed:**

Water absorption:
- Bread flour absorbs MORE water than all-purpose
- Start with recipe's specified water amount
- Add more gradually as needed to achieve proper dough consistency
- For every 1 cup bread flour, may need slightly more hydration

Dough behavior:
- Dough may develop more quickly than expected
- Monitor closely during fermentation and proofing
- May require shorter fermentation/proofing times

Handling:
- Bread flour dough can be more delicate
- Be GENTLE when shaping to avoid overworking
- Avoid developing too much tension in the dough
- Handle with care to preserve the open crumb structure

**Recipe-specific guidance:**

For focaccia:
- Don't overmix the dough
- Mix just until shaggy mass forms
- Let dough rest 10–15 minutes after initial mixing
- Don't over-proof — bake when lightly puffed
- Use high-quality extra-virgin olive oil

For sourdough:
- Use healthy, active starter at peak
- Maintain dough temperature 75°F–78°F (24°C–25°C)
- Use gentle mixing and folding (don't overmix)
- Aim for 70–80% hydration
- Use slow, gentle proofing
- Bake with steam for expanded crumb
- Score the bread before baking

**Learning point:** Bread flour is an excellent choice for experimentation but requires attention to hydration, fermentation timing, and gentle handling to achieve best results.
