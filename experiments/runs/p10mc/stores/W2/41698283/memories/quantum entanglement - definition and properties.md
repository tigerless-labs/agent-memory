## Quantum Entanglement

Quantum entanglement is a phenomenon where two or more particles become correlated in such a way that their individual properties are no longer independent of each other, even when separated by large distances.

When two particles are entangled (e.g., with opposite spins), measuring the state of one particle instantaneously determines the state of the other, regardless of the distance between them. This effect is sometimes called "spooky action at a distance" (Einstein's characterization).

Key points:
- The effect is instantaneous across arbitrarily large distances
- The particles remain correlated even after separation
- This is a fundamental aspect of quantum mechanics
- Experimentally confirmed
- Has practical applications in quantum cryptography and quantum computing
- Cannot be explained by classical physics
