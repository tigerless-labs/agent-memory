## Chapter 13: Rule #5 — Use the Power in Your Posterior

**Core Problem: Gluteal Amnesia**
Modern sedentary lifestyle causes glutes to atrophy from overuse as a "seat cushion." When glutes go to sleep:
- Brain loses connectivity with the muscles
- Spine takes over functions glutes were designed to handle
- Tiny spine muscles become strained and fail
- Forces transmit directly onto discs, joints, ligaments, tendons → serious damage
- Psoas muscles tighten reflexively, adding more pressure on spine

**Why This Matters**
The glutes are the largest muscles in the body. They should handle:
- Standing and maintaining erect posture
- Preventing forward fall during walking/running
- Keeping pelvis stable when standing on one leg
- Rising from seated position
- Picking things up, climbing stairs, running, skiing, rowing

Every repeated action (getting out of chair, picking up, tying shoes, stairs) puts tons of pressure on the spine it's not designed to bear. Over days/months/years, micro-irritations accumulate into macro damage ("geologic time").

**High Correlation:** Very high correlation between glutes going to sleep and back pain.

**Solution: Three Steps**
1. Wake up your glutes
2. Make them strong
3. Learn to use them at appropriate times (as designed)

**Exercises Mentioned**
- **Bridge** — foundational, may require modification if difficult
- **Clamshell** — critical for waking up glutes (often boring, often poorly performed)
  - Lie on side with hips/knees bent
  - Use thumb on top hand to dig inside upper hip bone
  - Lay four fingers across outside of hip
  - Middle finger (or first/third) should lie on gluteal muscles
  - Poke fingers into the muscles, feel them, reestablish brain connection
  - Find neutral spine and brace core

**Key Insight**
Once glutes wake up and do their intended work, the psoas muscles (opposite motion) reflexively loosen up.
