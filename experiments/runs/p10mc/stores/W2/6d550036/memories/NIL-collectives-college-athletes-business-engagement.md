## NIL Basics

**NIL** = Name, Image, and Likeness rights — allows college athletes to profit from their personal brand: name, image, social media following, endorsements, sponsorships.

**NCAA Reform (2021)**: NCAA adopted new rules allowing college athletes to profit from NIL without losing eligibility. Previously prohibited.

**Benefits for Athletes**:
- Earn money while in college
- Support themselves and families
- Control over personal brand
- Monetize social media and marketable skills

**Risks**:
- Managing finances
- Protecting intellectual property
- Avoiding NCAA violations
- Conflicts of interest

## Collectives

**Definition**: Groups of athletes who pool their NIL rights together to negotiate with third parties (brands, marketing agencies).

**Athlete Benefits**:
- Better negotiating power and leverage
- Centralized support on legal/financial matters
- Education on NIL rules
- Help avoiding NCAA violations

**Athletic Program Benefits**:
- Help athletes navigate NIL complexities
- Protect eligibility
- Provide monetization opportunities
- Attract and retain top talent

## Business Engagement Models

Collectives facilitate athlete-business engagement through:

1. **Sponsorship Deals** — athletes promote business products/services via social media, events, marketing
2. **Social Media Campaigns** — coordinate campaigns where athletes promote to their followers
3. **Endorsements** — athlete appearances in ads, product launches, promotions
4. **Events and Appearances** — book athletes for store openings, launches, promotional events
5. **Product Collaborations** — athletes design or co-develop product lines

## Key Dynamic

Businesses connect with athletes through collectives as a centralized platform; athletes avoid complex NIL negotiation; collectives benefit from coordinating multiple parties.
