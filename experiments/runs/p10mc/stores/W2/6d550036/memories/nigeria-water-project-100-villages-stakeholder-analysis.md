## Water Infrastructure Project in Nigeria

**Project**: Connecting 100 rural villages to running water infrastructure

### Stakeholders & Impact Measurement Framework

**Beneficiary Stakeholders**

1. **Rural communities in 100 villages** (Individuals)
   - Benefit: Improved access to clean water
   - Indicators: (1) Increase in access to safe, clean water sources (2) Reduction in water-borne illnesses (3) Increased time for productive activities (education, employment)

2. **Local government and authorities** (Government)
   - Benefit: Improved public health and reduced burden on local resources
   - Indicators: (1) Reduction in water-borne disease healthcare costs (2) Public satisfaction with water/sanitation services (3) Increased tax revenue from economic activity

3. **Water and sanitation organizations** (Non-profit)
   - Benefit: Expanded service reach
   - Indicators: (1) Increased access to safe water in rural communities (2) Reduction in water-borne illnesses (3) Enhanced reputation and credibility

4. **Health care providers and facilities** (Business/Non-profit)
   - Benefit: Improved public health, reduced disease burden
   - Indicators: (1) Reduction in cases of water-borne illnesses (2) Patient satisfaction increase (3) Revenue increase from reduced resource strain

5. **Agricultural businesses and farmers** (Business)
   - Benefit: Improved access to water for irrigation and farming
   - Indicators: (1) Crop yield and quality increase (2) Reduced water-related crop losses (3) Increased revenue

6. **Women and children who collect water** (Individuals)
   - Benefit: Reduced time and effort spent collecting water
   - Indicators: (1) Reduction in time spent collecting water (2) Increased time for education and employment (3) Improved health from reduced physical strain

7. **Schools and educational institutions** (Non-profit)
   - Benefit: Improved water access for students and staff
   - Indicators: (1) Increased access to safe water (2) Reduced water-borne illnesses among students/staff (3) Enhanced school reputation

8. **Local businesses and industries** (Business)
   - Benefit: Improved access to water for operations
   - Indicators: (1) Increased production capacity (2) Reduced water-related losses (3) Increased revenue

**Stakeholders Potentially Harmed**

1. **Landowners and residents who may be displaced** (Individuals)
   - Harm: Loss of property and displacement
   - Indicators: (1) Number of people displaced (2) Adequacy of compensation and support (3) Property value reduction

2. **Traditional water sources** (Environment/Community)
   - Harm: Contamination or obsolescence
   - Indicators: (1) Water quality reduction in traditional sources (2) Decreased use of traditional sources (3) Impact on religious/cultural practices

3. **Environmental organizations** (Non-profit)
   - Harm: Potential environmental impacts
   - Indicators: (1) Water quality reduction in surrounding environment (2) Negative impact on wildlife/habitats (3) Public support reduction for environmental orgs

4. **Small-scale water vendors** (Business)
   - Harm: Loss of business due to running water availability
   - Indicators: (1) Reduction in water sales (2) Revenue decrease (3) Loss of livelihood

### Key Considerations

- Analysis identifies 12 stakeholder groups with detailed measurement frameworks
- Captures both positive and negative externalities
- Enables monitoring of project equity and inclusive impact
- Supports identification of compensation needs and mitigation strategies for harmed stakeholders
