**William Henry Fox Talbot (1800-1877)**

English scientist, mathematician, and photographer. Made significant contributions to photography development, particularly the calotype process.

**Key Contributions:**

1. **Calotype Process**
   - Paper-based photographic process
   - Used paper coated with silver chloride
   - Created negative image
   - Negative could be used to create multiple positive prints
   - Revolutionary innovation for mass production

2. **Negative-Positive Process**
   - Introduced concept of negative-positive system
   - Negative image used to create multiple positive prints
   - Major innovation enabling mass production of photographs
   - Fundamental principle underlying all modern film photography

3. **First Photographic Print**
   - Produced first photographic print in 1841
   - Title: "The Pencil of Nature"
   - Showed latticed window at Lacock Abbey
   - Demonstrated capability of paper-based process

4. **Chemical & Material Improvements**
   - Experimented with various papers and chemicals
   - Led to improvements in sensitivity of photographic materials
   - Improved stability of photographic prints

5. **Calotype Process Steps:**
   - Prepare paper: coat with silver chloride and gallic acid
   - Dry and sensitize in dark
   - Expose in camera for several minutes
   - Remove from camera
   - Develop with gallic acid and silver nitrate solution
   - Fix with sodium chloride to remove unexposed silver
   - Rinse with water
   - Create positive: place second silver chloride coated paper in contact with negative
   - Expose both to light
   - Second sheet becomes positive print

**Historical Impact:**
While less commercially successful than Daguerre's process initially, Talbot's negative-positive approach became the foundation for all subsequent photographic processes. Professional photographers preferred daguerreotypy initially, but Talbot's innovation proved more influential long-term.

**Comparison:**
- Daguerre focused on portraiture; Talbot on landscape and still life
- Daguerre more commercially successful initially
- Talbot's negative-positive concept became the standard approach
