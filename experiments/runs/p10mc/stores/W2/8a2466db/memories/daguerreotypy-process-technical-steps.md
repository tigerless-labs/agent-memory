**Daguerreotypy Technical Process**

The photographic process invented by Louis Daguerre in 1839, first commercially successful photography method.

**Step 1: Prepare the Plate**
- Start with copper plate
- Polish to high shine using increasingly fine abrasives
- Wash and dry to remove residue

**Step 2: Coat the Plate**
- Apply thin layer of silver via electroplating
- Sensitize silver layer with iodine vapor to make light-sensitive

**Step 3: Expose the Plate**
- Place plate in camera
- Focus camera on subject
- Open shutter to expose plate to light
- Exposure time: several minutes (varies with brightness)

**Step 4: Develop the Plate**
- Remove plate from camera
- Treat with mercury vapor
- Mercury reacts with silver particles to create positive image

**Step 5: Fix and Rinse**
- Treat with fixing agent such as sodium chloride
- Removes unexposed silver particles
- Rinse with water to remove remaining chemicals

**Step 6: Tone and Seal**
- Tone with gold chloride to enhance image and add warm tone
- Seal with varnish to protect from fading

**Key Characteristics:**
- Produces positive image directly (no negative)
- Image is permanent and highly detailed
- One-of-a-kind image (cannot be reproduced like Talbot's process)
- Requires careful handling of toxic mercury vapor
- Popular primarily for portraiture 1839-1860s
- Gradually replaced by calotype and later processes
