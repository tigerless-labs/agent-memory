**Calotypy Technical Process**

The photographic process invented by William Henry Fox Talbot in 1841, revolutionary paper-based negative-positive process.

**Step 1: Prepare the Paper**
- Coat sheet of paper with solution of silver chloride and gallic acid
- Dry the coated paper
- Sensitize in dark

**Step 2: Expose the Paper (Creating Negative)**
- Place paper in camera
- Focus camera on subject
- Open shutter to expose paper to light
- Exposure time: several minutes (varies with brightness and desired detail level)

**Step 3: Develop the Paper**
- Remove paper from camera
- Treat with solution of gallic acid and silver nitrate
- Solution brings out image, creating negative image on paper

**Step 4: Fix and Rinse**
- Treat with fixing agent such as sodium chloride
- Removes unexposed silver particles
- Rinse with water to remove remaining chemicals

**Step 5: Create Positive Print (From Negative)**
- Coat second sheet of paper with silver chloride solution
- Place second sheet in contact with negative image
- Expose both sheets to light
- Positive image created on second sheet

**Key Characteristics:**
- Two-step process: negative then positive
- Paper-based (versus Daguerre's metal plate)
- Can create multiple prints from single negative
- Revolutionary for mass production of photographs
- Foundation for all modern photographic printing
- Less harsh toxic requirements than mercury vapor
- Suitable for landscape and still-life photography
- Less commercially successful than daguerreotypes initially
- Became the standard approach for photography long-term
