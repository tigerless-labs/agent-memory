**Louis Daguerre (1787-1851)**

French artist and photographer credited with inventing the daguerreotype, the first commercially successful photographic process.

**Key Contributions:**

1. **Daguerreotype Process**
   - Used copper plate coated with silver
   - Plate exposed to light to capture image
   - Resulted in positive, highly detailed, permanent photograph

2. **First Permanent Photograph**
   - Took first permanent photograph August 19, 1826
   - Title: "View from the Window at Le Gras"
   - Showed courtyard and surrounding buildings of his estate

3. **Process Improvements**
   - Reduced exposure times compared to earlier attempts
   - Introduced mercury vapor to develop images
   - Mercury vapor reacted with silver particles to create positive image
   - Developed first photographic camera specifically for daguerreotypy

4. **Popularization & Commercial Success**
   - Made photography accessible to masses
   - Daguerre's Paris studio became popular destination for portraits
   - Over 1 million units sold between 1926-1940 (note: this date range may be for camera models, not daguerreotype era)
   - Process became primary photographic method mid-1800s

5. **Process Steps:**
   - Prepare/polish copper plate
   - Coat with silver layer via electroplating
   - Sensitize with iodine vapor
   - Expose in camera
   - Develop with mercury vapor
   - Fix with sodium chloride
   - Tone with gold chloride
   - Seal with varnish

**Historical Impact:**
Daguerre's process dominated early photography and was the primary method until the calotype and later processes emerged.
