## Ursula K. Le Guin: Major Ecocritical Works

Ursula K. Le Guin is a celebrated science fiction and fantasy author known for exploring ecological, social, and political issues through her works.

### Major Works

1. **"The Dispossessed" (1974)** — Tells story of anarchists establishing commune on distant planet; explores anarchism as political/social system; notable for exploration of human-environment relationship and critique of capitalist ideologies
2. **"The Left Hand of Darkness" (1969)** — Set on planet with genderless inhabitants; explores themes of gender, sexuality, and power; notable for exploration of human-environment relationship and critique of societal attitudes toward nature
3. **"The Word for World is Forest" (1976)** — Story of conflict between human colonizers and native inhabitants of a planet; explores themes of imperialism, colonialism, and human-environment relationship
4. **"The Lathe of Heaven" (1971)** — Explores intersection of science fiction and environmentalism through story of man whose dreams change reality; critiques destructive human impact on environment and misuse of science/technology
5. **"Always Coming Home" (1985)** — Set in post-apocalyptic California; explores environmentalism, ecology, and human-environment relationship through collection of stories, songs, and poetry

### Influence

Le Guin is known for ability to create compelling stories exploring complex social and political issues, critique of capitalist ideologies, societal attitudes toward nature, and destructive human impact on the environment. Her works have been influential in science fiction, fantasy, and literature generally.
