## Ethical Concerns: AI in Insurance

### Main Risk Areas

1. **Biased Training Data**
   - AI algorithms are only as unbiased as their training data
   - If historical data is biased (excludes certain populations, overrepresents others), algorithm produces biased outcomes
   - Example: historical dataset that disproportionately excludes certain races/ethnicities → discriminatory practices

2. **Reinforcement of Existing Societal Biases**
   - Risk assessment algorithms based on past claims data may inadvertently reinforce existing biases
   - Assumptions that certain neighborhoods, occupations, or demographics are "high-risk" can discriminate against individuals in those groups
   - Creates feedback loop: biased assumptions → discriminatory decisions → biased future data

3. **Lack of Transparency**
   - Some AI algorithms are opaque with poor interpretability
   - Cannot explain reasons behind decisions
   - Raises concerns about accountability, trust, and fairness

### Mitigation Measures

Organizations should take steps to ensure AI algorithms are:
- **Unbiased**: review training data, test for bias
- **Transparent**: establish clear guidelines for decision-making
- **Accountable**: involve diverse stakeholders in design and implementation

**Diverse stakeholder involvement** is essential — include those who may be affected by algorithm decisions in design and implementation process.
