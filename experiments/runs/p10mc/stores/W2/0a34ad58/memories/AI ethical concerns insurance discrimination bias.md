## Key Ethical Concerns

**Bias in training data**: AI algorithms are only as unbiased as their training data. If historical data disproportionately represents or excludes certain groups (by race, ethnicity, location, etc.), the algorithm will produce biased outcomes and discriminatory practices.

**Reinforcement of societal biases**: Algorithms designed to identify high-risk individuals based on past claims data may inadvertently reinforce existing societal biases by assuming certain neighborhoods, occupations, or demographics are inherently high-risk, discriminating against specific groups.

**Lack of transparency**: Many AI algorithms are opaque and lack interpretability to explain their decisions. This raises concerns about accountability, trust, and fairness when decisions affecting people's insurance eligibility or pricing cannot be understood or challenged.

## Mitigation Measures

- Review training data for bias
- Test algorithms for discriminatory outcomes
- Establish clear guidelines and protocols for AI use
- Involve diverse stakeholders, especially affected populations, in algorithm design
- Invest in transparency and explainability of AI decisions
- Ensure data diversity and absence of bias in models
