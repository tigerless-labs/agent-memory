## Games & Activities to Help Toddler Feel Included with New Baby

### Games Involving the New Baby
- **Baby's First... Guessing Game** — Child predicts baby's milestones (first smile, laugh, tooth, coo, etc.). Write down predictions and check them off as they happen. Great for creating investment in baby's development.
- **Babydoll Care Role-Play** — Provide doll or stuffed animal for child to practice caring (feeding, burping, rocking). Models healthy caregiving behavior.
- **New Baby Scavenger Hunt** — Hide pictures or toys related to babies around house. Child searches and brings items to designated baby area/nursery.
- **Big Brother/Sister Helper** — Create simple task list (fetch diapers, bring toys, get blankets for mom). Makes child feel important and included in baby care.
- **Baby's Feeding Time** — Practice feeding baby doll with toy bottle or pacifier. Helps child understand baby feeding routines.

### Regular Activities (Not Baby-Focused)
- **Treasure Hunts** — Hide toys or treats around house to create excitement and positive distraction
- **Arts & Crafts** — Paper, glue, scissors, markers for creative expression
- **Obstacle Courses** — Use household items (couch cushions, chairs, blankets) for crawling, jumping, climbing
- **Cooking or Baking** — Age-appropriate recipes where child helps measure, mix, assemble
- **Music & Dance Parties** — Put on fun music and dance together; make instruments from pots/pans
- **Nature Walks** — Explore neighborhood or park, observe nature, collect items
- **Backyard Games** — Soccer, basketball, running, small trampoline play
- **Story Time** — Read books or create stories together with fun voices and sound effects
- **Dress-Up & Imaginative Play** — Costumes, props, pretend restaurant/store/adventure scenarios
- **Movie Nights** — Special movie with favorite film, popcorn, snuggles
- **Picnics** — Set up special picnic indoors or outdoors with favorite foods

## Tips for Success
- **Follow child's lead** — let them choose activities and take the lead
- **Be present** — engage fully, put away phone and distractions
- **Encourage imagination** — provide open-ended materials, foster creative thinking
- **Take breaks** — don't overexert child, rest when needed
- **Communicate with parent** — keep parent informed about activities, ask for input
- **Teach empathy** — encourage child to consider baby's feelings and needs
- **Be patient** — child may need time to adjust, jealousy/frustration is normal
- **Maintain routine** — stick to child's regular schedule including mealtimes, playtime, bedtime
- **Give one-on-one time** — ensure child still feels special and loved
- **Positive reinforcement** — praise gentle interactions with baby ("You're being so gentle!")
- **Keep it simple** — start with simple activities, increase complexity over time
