## 19th Century Korea: Western and Catholic Persecutions

### Major Events

**1866 Byeong-in Persecution**
- Widespread crackdown on Catholicism in Korea
- Resulted in killing of thousands of Catholics
- Included Western priests and laypeople

**1871 Shinmiyangyo Incident**
- American sailors killed by Korean soldiers
- First armed confrontation between United States and Korea
- Heightened tensions between the two countries

**1886 Massacre in Gaeseong**
- Hundreds of Korean Catholics killed
- Father Thomas Hur (French priest) among victims
- Kim Ok-gyun (intellectual/government official) critical of government response

### Pattern and Context

These killings and persecutions were not isolated events but part of larger pattern of violence against Westerners and Catholics in Korea during 19th century.

**Root Causes:**
- Growing influence of Western powers in Korea
- Government determination to maintain power and control
- Resistance to foreign religious and cultural influence
- Tension between traditional Confucian order and Western ideas

### Outcome

Despite these persecutions, Catholicism continued to spread in Korea. By 20th century, it had become one of largest and most influential religious communities in country.

**Legacy:** Persecutions remain important part of Korea's history, reminder of Korean people's determination to maintain independence and cultural identity.
