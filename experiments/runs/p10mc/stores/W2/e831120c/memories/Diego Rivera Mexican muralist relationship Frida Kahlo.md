## Diego Rivera and His Relationship with Frida

**Diego Rivera's Career and Style**
- Leading figure in Mexican Muralist movement
- Prominent painter, muralist, and communist activist
- Artistic style: realism, social commentary, symbolism, mythology
- Famous works: murals at Palacio de Bellas Artes (Mexico City) and Detroit Industry Murals (Detroit Institute of Arts)
- Focus on lives and struggles of working class, industrialization, and capitalism

**Marriage and Romantic Relationship**
- Married Frida Kahlo twice: 1929-1939 and 1940 until Frida's death in 1954
- Relationship marked by intense passion, mutual admiration, deep artistic connection
- Characterized by infidelity, disagreements, periods of separation
- Turbulent nature fueled their artistic creativity

**Artistic Influence and Collaboration**
- Diego influenced Frida's use of symbolism and mythological references
- Frida adopted many of his symbolic motifs drawing from Mexican folklore and Aztec mythology
- Shared emphasis on promoting Mexican culture and identity
- Frida assisted Diego on his murals; Diego encouraged Frida's artistic development
- Mutual inspiration — Diego influenced by Frida's exploration of more personal, introspective themes

**Reflected in Frida's Art**
- The Two Fridas (1939) — depicts complexities and contradictions of their love
- Self-Portrait with Thorn Necklace and Hummingbird (1940) — expresses emotional pain from relationship
