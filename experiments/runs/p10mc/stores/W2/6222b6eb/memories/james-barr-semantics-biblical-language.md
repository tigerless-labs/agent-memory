## Overview
James Barr's "The Semantics of Biblical Language" (Oxford University Press, 1961) is a seminal work in biblical studies and linguistics.

## Main Premise
The book argues that accurate interpretation of the Bible requires a **nuanced and contextualized understanding** of its language, rejecting earlier simplistic and overly literal approaches.

## Key Arguments
- Words and meanings cannot be understood in isolation; context is essential
- The semantic range of words must be considered
- Metaphor, idiom, and figurative language are crucial to understanding biblical meaning
- Biblical Hebrew and Greek were not static but constantly evolving languages
- Historical, cultural, and literary context must inform interpretation
- Language itself is fluid and contextual — meanings change over time and usage
- A sophisticated linguistic approach yields more accurate interpretations than literal translation
