Best practices for recording instrument performances (ukulele, other instruments):\n\n**Audio recording:**\n- Use dedicated condenser microphone (e.g., Blue Yeti, Rode NT-USB)\n- Position microphone 6-12 inches away, slightly off-center, angled toward sound hole\n- Use pop filter to reduce plosive sounds\n- Record in quiet room with minimal echo/reverb (closets, padded rooms work well)\n- Use DAW software: Audacity (free), GarageBand, Adobe Audition\n\n**Video recording:**\n- Use natural light or quality lighting kit/softbox lights\n- Good camera with image stabilization (smartphone acceptable)\n- Use tripod for stable footage\n- Connect external microphone for better audio capture (lavalier or shotgun)\n- Frame from multiple angles to show instrument and hands\n\n**Content preparation:**\n- Plan arrangement and practice before recording\n- Choose clear, recognizable song (cover recommended for first videos)\n- Keep videos 2-3 minutes for engagement\n- Introduce yourself and song at start\n- Show personality and enthusiasm\n\n**Post-production:**\n- Use editing software: iMovie, Adobe Premiere Pro, DaVinci Resolve\n- Add captions and tags for discoverability\n- Trim, cut, and arrange clips\n\n**First video tips:**\n- Cover song easier than tutorial for beginners\n- Less intimidating, more engaging for viewers\n- Builds audience connection
