Straight photography is a style that emerged in the early 20th century (particularly 1920s-1930s) characterized by straightforward, objective approach without manipulation or artistic effects. 

**Key figures:**
- **Alfred Stieglitz** — photographer and critic who coined the term "straight photography"; believed photography should be treated as an art form in its own right while maintaining honesty and truthfulness
- **Edward Weston** — famous straight photographer
- **Paul Strand** — famous straight photographer
- **Ansel Adams** — famous straight photographer

**Characteristics:**
- Truthful, honest, objective representations of the world
- Rejected artificial effects or manipulation
- Simple, everyday subjects (landscapes, still lifes, portraits)
- Clarity, precision, attention to detail
- Influenced by Pictorialism and documentary tradition, but rejected their use of embellishments

**Legacy:** Influence seen in modern street photography, landscape, and portrait photography; celebrated for commitment to objectivity and truthfulness.
