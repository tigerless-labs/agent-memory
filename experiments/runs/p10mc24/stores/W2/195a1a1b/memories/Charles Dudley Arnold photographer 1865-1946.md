**Charles Dudley Arnold** (1865-1946) was an American photographer known for pioneering work in aerial and nighttime photography.

**Early career:**
- Born in New York City
- Studied at Art Students League of New York
- Began as portrait photographer
- Became interested in capturing images from unusual perspectives

**Aerial photography pioneer:**
- 1890s: experimented with aerial photography using camera mounted on balloons
- Early 1900s: used airplanes as platform, becoming one of first photographers to do so
- Worked with pioneering aviators, including **Orville Wright** (Wright brothers)
- Used variety of cameras and techniques
- Photographs characterized by innovative composition, ability to capture landscape patterns and textures from unique perspective
- Helped establish aerial photography as new and important genre; inspired other photographers

**Nighttime photography:**
- Also known for innovative nighttime photography (unusual for his time)
- Late 19th/early 20th century: photography was primarily daytime due to technology limitations
- Used artificial light sources: electric lights, gas lamps, torches
- Developed special equipment: **flash powder** and **magnesium wire** to produce bright light bursts for darkness photography
- Often photographed urban scenes — city streets and buildings with dramatic, moody, atmospheric effect
- Images characterized by sharp contrasts between light and dark, capturing mystery and drama of night
- Pioneering effort that influenced other photographers experimenting with nighttime photography

**Legacy:**
- Aerial and nighttime photographs recognized as important historical documents and works of art
- Continue to inspire and influence photographers worldwide
