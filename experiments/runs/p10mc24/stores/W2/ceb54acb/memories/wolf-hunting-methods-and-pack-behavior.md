## Wolf Hunting Methods

**Primary hunting methods:**
- Pursuit hunting: chase prey for miles until exhausted
- Ambush hunting: hide and surprise prey
- Pack hunting: cooperative behavior using superior numbers
- Stealth hunting: stalk prey, moving low to ground
- Opportunistic hunting: scavenge or hunt smaller prey (rabbits, rodents, birds) when large prey unavailable

**Prey and Diet:**
- Wolves are apex predators with specialized carnivorous digestive systems
- Primary prey: elk, deer, bison, moose, caribou (herbivorous animals)
- Diet based on **availability**, not preference
- In winter, may shift to smaller prey (hares, voles) when large herbivores scarce
- Do not eat vegetarian; occasionally supplement with fruits, berries, grasses but not as primary food source

## Solo vs Pack Hunting

- Wolves **can hunt alone** but are significantly less successful
- Solo hunting typically targets smaller, weaker prey only
- Solo hunting is riskier—wolves can be injured or killed by large prey or other predators
- Pack hunting allows wolves to better utilize their strengths and minimize risk

## Pack Coordination During Hunts

**Pre-hunt planning:**
- Wolves communicate via vocalizations, physical gestures, scents
- Coordinate strategy based on prey size/strength, pack size, terrain

**Execution strategies:**
- Coordinate movements to surround prey and push into vulnerable position
- Take turns chasing to tire prey while others wait in ambush
- Use ambush strategy with hiding in vegetation
- Division of labor: some wolves better at pursuit, others at ambush/takedown

**After the kill:**
- Alpha male and female typically eat first
- Hierarchy determines food distribution order
- If prey large enough, all pack members can eat; smaller kills require turn-taking
- Individual personalities and dominance behavior affect actual distribution

## Conflict Resolution

- Conflicts arise over resources, individual personalities, social dynamics
- Resolved through **hierarchy and dominance/submission behaviors**
- Common conflict signals: growling, snarling, biting
- Lower-ranking individual typically submits to higher-ranking
- Most conflicts resolved without serious injury
- During hunts, alpha and dominant wolves lead; others wait their turn
- Food sharing can escalate to physical aggression if resources scarce, but usually resolved through hierarchy

**Pack structure:** Wolves maintain complex social hierarchy through physical posturing, vocalizations, physical contact (nipping/biting)
