## Repudiation Definition

**Two-Part Legal Test**: A party shows through conduct that either:
1. It no longer intends to be bound by the contract, OR
2. It intends to fulfill the contract only in a way substantially inconsistent with obligations

## Examples of Repudiation by Builder (Homeowner's right to terminate)
- Significantly delayed with work
- Refusing to build the contract scope of work
- Demanding payment for more money than entitled to

## Examples of Repudiation by Owner (Builder's right to terminate)
- Owner notifies Builder that Builder must carry out more work when not contractually obliged
- Owner demands Builder charge less than contractually obliged
- Owner conducts self in manner preventing Builder from fulfilling obligations

## Key Distinction: Repudiation vs Breach
Repudiation involves more than ordinary breach. Conduct must show **clear intention** by other party to effectively walk away from agreement. Requires compelling evidence to support termination for repudiation.

## Rights if Other Party Repudiates
1. Accept repudiation and elect to terminate the contract, OR
2. Affirm the contract and insist other party continue to perform

In construction context, most people elect to terminate (option 1).

## Consequences of Wrongful Termination
If you terminate without right, this can amount to repudiation itself. Consequences:
- Other party can elect to terminate contract
- Other party can sue you for damages
- Requires careful consideration and legal advice before terminating
