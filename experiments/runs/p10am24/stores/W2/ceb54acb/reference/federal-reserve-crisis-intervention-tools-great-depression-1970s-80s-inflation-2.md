Historical examples of Federal Reserve intervention during economic crises:

**Great Depression (1929-1933):** The Fed initially raised rates (contributing to severity), then in 1933 shifted to expansionary policy with sharp rate reductions and large-scale government securities purchases.

**1970s-1980s Inflation:** The Fed used various tools including adjusting bank reserve requirements and open market operations to tighten monetary policy and bring inflation under control.

**2007-2009 Financial Crisis:** Unprecedented steps included creation of new lending facilities, large-scale purchases of government bonds and other securities, and reduction of interest rates to near-zero levels.

**COVID-19 Pandemic (2020):** Included reduction in interest rates, creation of new lending facilities, and large-scale purchases of securities to provide liquidity to financial markets.

These tools demonstrate the Fed's ability to support the economy during severe disruptions through both traditional (interest rate adjustments) and unconventional (quantitative easing, lending facilities) measures.
