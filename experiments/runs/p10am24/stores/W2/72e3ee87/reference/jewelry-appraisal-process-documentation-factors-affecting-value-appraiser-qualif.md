**Documentation to Provide for Jewelry Appraisal:**
1. Proof of ownership (will, trust, inheritance papers)
2. Provenance: historical records, receipts, certificates, letters about origins
3. Repair and maintenance records with receipts
4. Any existing appraisals or certificates
5. Clear, high-quality photos and videos from multiple angles

**Factors That Affect Appraisal Value:**
1. Type and quality of diamonds: carat weight, cut, color, clarity
2. Metal type and quality: gold, platinum, silver and their purity
3. Craftsmanship and design: era of creation
4. Condition: damage, wear, needed repairs
5. Rarity and uniqueness: one-of-a-kind or historical significance
6. Market demand: current market for similar pieces
7. Comparable sales: research of similar pieces sold

**Appraiser Qualifications to Look For:**
- Certification from reputable organizations (NAJA, AGTA, ASA)
- Experience with similar items, especially vintage jewelry
- Independent (not affiliated with jewelry stores)

**What to Expect in Appraisal Report:**
1. Detailed description: materials, dimensions, characteristics
2. Appraiser's opinion of value (may include range)
3. Explanation of appraisal methodology
4. Supporting photos and diagrams

**Appraisal Organizations:**
- NAJA: National Association of Jewelry Appraisers
- AGTA: American Gem Trade Association
- ASA: American Society of Appraisers
- AAA: Appraisers Association of America (antiques, art, collectibles)
- ISA: International Society of Appraisers
- NACA: National Association of Collectible Appraisers
