Straight photography emerged in the 1920s-1930s as a straightforward, objective approach to photography without manipulation or artistic effects.

**Key Principles:**
- Commitment to objectivity, truthfulness, and honest representation
- Rejection of artificial effects or manipulation
- Treated photography as art form in its own right

**Origins:**
Coined/developed by Alfred Stieglitz, who believed photography should maintain honesty while being treated as fine art. Influenced by Pictorialism and documentary tradition but rejected their artificiality.

**Notable Photographers:**
- Edward Weston
- Paul Strand  
- Ansel Adams

Often featured simple, everyday subjects (landscapes, still lifes, portraits) approached with clarity, precision, and attention to detail. Continues to influence street photography, landscape, and portrait photography today.
