Paul Strand (1890-1976) was an American photographer and pioneer of straight photography and modernist photography.

**Career:**
- Born in New York City, largely self-taught
- Influenced by Alfred Stieglitz; joined Photo-Secession in 1915
- Shifted to documentary photography in 1920s, focusing on everyday life and social issues
- Traveled extensively photographing rural communities, urban landscapes, industrial sites

**Major Work:**
- Famous series of photographs from Mexico (began 1932)
- Considered some of the most important examples of documentary photography from the period
- Simple, direct style with sense of social purpose

**Philosophy:**
Deeply committed to using photography as a powerful tool for social change—raising awareness of social injustices and inspiring action. Work celebrated for innovative style, social relevance, and influence on modern photography.
