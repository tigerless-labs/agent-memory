Charles Dudley Arnold (1865-1946) was an American photographer known for pioneering work in aerial and nighttime photography.

**Background:**
- Born in New York City
- Studied at Art Students League of New York
- Started as portrait photographer before pursuing innovative perspectives

**Aerial Photography:**
- Began experimenting with aerial photography in 1890s using cameras mounted on balloons
- Early 1900s: used airplanes as photography platform, one of first photographers to do so
- Worked with pioneering aviators including Orville Wright
- Developed various cameras and techniques to capture landscape patterns and textures from above

**Nighttime Photography:**
- Unusual for his era when photography was primarily daytime-based
- Used artificial light sources: electric lights, gas lamps, torches
- Developed special equipment: flash powder, magnesium wire for light bursts
- Often photographed urban scenes (streets, buildings) with dramatic, moody, atmospheric effect
- Sharp contrasts between light and dark

**Legacy:**
Pioneered both aerial and nighttime photography as genres. Work recognized as important historical documents and art, continuing to influence photographers worldwide.
