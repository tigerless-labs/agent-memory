Glaze Composition: Glazes are mixtures of silicates, aluminosilicates, and metal oxides that determine color, opacity, and durability. Application methods include brushing, pouring, dipping, or spraying, each requiring different techniques and glaze consistency. Glaze thickness affects results - too thin means inadequate coverage, too thick causes running or pooling. Firing Types: Oxidation, reduction, raku, and salt/soda firings each require specific conditions. Glazes mature at temperatures between 1800F (980C) and 2400F (1300C). Firing atmosphere (neutral/oxidizing or reducing) affects glaze color and texture. Food-Safe Glazing: Choose glazes labeled 'food-safe' or 'lead-free' meeting safety standards. Test glazes on small non-functional pieces first. Monitor kiln temperature and atmosphere for consistent results. Follow manufacturer instructions for application, drying, and firing. Keep a glaze journal recording tests, application methods, and firing conditions to track progress and refine techniques.
