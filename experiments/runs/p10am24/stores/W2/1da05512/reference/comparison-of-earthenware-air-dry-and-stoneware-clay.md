Earthenware Clay: Soft and pliable (easy for beginners), fires at low temperature around 1800F/980C, wide range of colorful glaze options, but brittle and prone to cracking/breaking, not suitable for functional ware (not food-safe or durable for everyday use). Air-Dry Clay: No kiln required, fast results without lengthy firing, easy to repair by re-moistening and reshaping, but limited durability and prone to cracking/breaking, not suitable for outdoor use, best for small and simple projects. Stoneware Clay: Strong and durable for functional ware and outdoor use, fires at high temperature around 2400F/1300C making it resistant to thermal shock, non-porous and food-safe/watertight for cups/plates/bowls, but more difficult to work with than other clays, requires high-temperature kiln access, and limited glaze options due to high-temperature requirements.
